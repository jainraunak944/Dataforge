## SLIDE 1 — BOARD RECOMMENDATION

### Buy a 26% strategic stake and a call option now; do not buy 100% of Liminal today

**RECOMMENDATION**
Do **not** acquire 100% of Liminal for ₹4,000 Cr today. Instead:

1. **Invest ₹1,040 Cr in cash for a 26% stake** (TA: pro-rata to the ₹4,000 Cr headline, treated as a ceiling; 26% = one share above the 25% special-resolution threshold, i.e. negative control without operational control).
2. **Sign a commercial custody/MPC partnership** for Paytm's tokenized-treasury, e₹ and cross-border settlement use cases.
3. **Ring-fence Liminal**: standalone brand, arm's-length board, information barriers, independent compliance.
4. **Hold a pre-agreed call option on the remaining 74%**, exercisable months 12–24, at a price capped at ₹2,960 Cr and linked to retained ARR (₹1,650 Cr upfront + up to ₹1,310 Cr retention earn-out).

**WHY NOT 100% TODAY — four numbers the Board should hold**

| Pressure | Case fact / calc | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Client revolt | 30–45% of ARR lost within 12 months of full acquisition (CF) | Effective price rises from 18.2× to 26–33× retained ARR (CALC) |

| Breakeven | ₹750 Cr drag vs ₹115 Cr MDR (CF) | Full-acquisition FCF does not break even within 10 years in any scenario; MDR offsets at most 15% of the drag (CALC) |

**BOARD ASK**

## SLIDE 2 — STRATEGIC RATIONALE

### Liminal extends Paytm from payments into institutional digital-settlement infrastructure — but access, not ownership, captures most of that value

**PAYTM TODAY (CF):** UPI · merchant payments · consumer financial services; ₹1,18,750 Cr market cap; UPI growth slowing, PhonePe and Google Pay dominate volume; regulatory scrutiny since RBI's 2024 Paytm Payments Bank restrictions; stated ambition in tokenized treasury and cross-border settlement aligned with RBI's e₹ pilot.

**LIMINAL ADDS (CF):** institutional digital-asset custody and MPC infrastructure (founded 2021, ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type II); clients are exchanges, hedge funds, OTC desks; HDFC Bank, Axis Bank, CoinDCX and ZebPay = 60% of ₹220 Cr ARR.

**STRATEGIC BRIDGE**
Payments → Institutional custody & MPC → Tokenized assets / treasury → e₹ / CBDC settlement rails → B2B & cross-border settlement

**WHY THIS MATTERS TO PAYTM**
- Custody/MPC is the trust layer every tokenized-settlement product needs; certifications and institutional trust take years to build in-house (TA).
- It is adjacent to, not a replacement for, the core: it gives the B2B settlement ambition a regulated-grade foundation.
- It is a hedge: if e₹/tokenized settlement scales, Paytm holds the rail; if it does not, the cheque is ₹1,040 Cr, not ₹4,000 Cr.

**KEY QUESTION** — Could a commercial or licensing partnership deliver the same outcome without ownership?
**OUR VIEW** — Mostly yes for the *capability*; no for *security of supply*. A pure partnership leaves Liminal open to the PhonePe–Mastercard consortium, after which Paytm's critical custody supplier would be owned by its main competitor. The 26% stake + ROFR + call option buys the blocking right and the information rights to run the validation; the partnership captures the capability. Together they cost 26% of the headline price.

**THE HONEST TRADE-OFF** — On a pure earnings basis, no structure is accretive before year 5; this is a strategic-positioning investment, and the cheque is sized accordingly.

## SLIDE 3 — THE OWNERSHIP PARADOX

### Full ownership impairs the asset Paytm is paying for — and the capital drag alone is worth more than the company

**₹4,000 Cr FULL ACQUISITION CREATES FOUR IMMEDIATE PRESSURES**

**1. Customer conflict (CF → CALC)**
Top-4 clients = ₹132 Cr of ₹220 Cr ARR. Case assumes ₹66–99 Cr of ARR (30–45%) is lost within 12 months, i.e. 50–75% of the top-4 book.

**2. Regulatory capital (CF → CALC)**
₹750 Cr/yr recurring FCF drag = 117% of Paytm EBITDA, 156% of PAT, 3.4× Liminal's entire revenue. To self-fund the drag, Liminal would need ~₹4,000 Cr ARR (18× today) at a 25% EBITDA margin — 16 years away at 20% growth, 11 at 30% (TA).

**3. Earnings (CF → TA)**
Liminal is ~PAT breakeven, so the deal brings no earnings to offset its own cost. Year-1 EPS at the case's ₹2,100 Cr cash + ₹1,900 Cr equity mix: ₹5.29 (−29%) after foregone treasury income (₹94.5 Cr) and a ₹41 Cr post-churn Liminal loss; ₹6.08 (−19%) even at zero churn.

**4. Valuation (CALC)**
₹4,000 Cr ÷ ₹220 Cr = 18.2× headline ARR → 26.0× / 29.1× / 33.1× retained ARR at 30 / 37.5 / 45% churn. Every 10 points of churn destroys ₹400 Cr of value at the paid multiple. Paytm itself trades at ~247× trailing earnings (CALC) — a −29% EPS print invites the buyback-versus-M&A debate the case warns about.

**CORE DEAL PARADOX** — Paytm wants Liminal for its institutional franchise; taking control is what breaks that franchise and triggers the capital requirement.

**BOARD IMPLICATION** — Separate the *strategic attractiveness of Liminal* (high) from the *economic attractiveness of owning 100% today* (negative under the case's own assumptions).

## SLIDE 5 — RECOMMENDED TRANSACTION ARCHITECTURE

### Pay ₹1,040 Cr for access and an option today; pay for control only against retained ARR, and never more than ₹4,000 Cr in total

**PHASE 1 — STRATEGIC ENTRY (TA)**
- **26% stake for ₹1,040 Cr** — secondary purchase from existing shareholders at pro-rata of the ₹4,000 Cr headline (ceiling; a minority discount is the negotiating objective). Funded 100% from balance-sheet cash: no new debt, no new shares. Cash after: ₹7,860 Cr.
- **Why 26%, not 25%:** one share above 25% blocks special resolutions (preferential allotment to a third party, charter changes, schemes of arrangement) — negative control that protects the option without operational control that would trigger the client revolt or the capital treatment.
- **Shareholder rights:** 1 of 5 board seats + observer; information and audit rights; ROFR/ROFO on all transfers; tag-along; anti-dilution; reserved matters (new investor, sale of business, change of custody model, related-party dealings with Paytm).

**+ COMMERCIAL PARTNERSHIP** — Multi-year custody/MPC services agreement for Paytm's tokenized-treasury, e₹ and cross-border pilots, on arm's-length terms, served by a segregated desk (Slide 10).

**+ GOVERNANCE RING-FENCE** — Standalone brand, independent chair, information barriers, independent compliance and cyber reporting, client charter (Slide 10).

**+ CALL OPTION ON THE REMAINING 74% (TA)**
- Window: months 12–24 after Phase 1 close; right, not obligation. After month 24 the option lapses; ROFR/ROFO persist.
- **Price cap ₹2,960 Cr** (= 74% of headline), paid as **₹1,650 Cr at exercise + retention earn-out of up to ₹1,310 Cr** at month 18 post-control, linear between 55% and 100% retained ARR:

| Post-control churn | Retained ARR | Earn-out | Control total | Grand total | 74% of 18.2× retained value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 100% | ₹1,310 | ₹2,960 | ₹4,000 | ₹2,960 |
| 20% | 80% | ₹728 | ₹2,378 | ₹3,418 | ₹2,368 |
| 30% | 70% | ₹437 | ₹2,087 | ₹3,127 | ₹2,072 |
| 45%+ | ≤55% | ₹0 | ₹1,650 | ₹2,690 | ₹1,628 |

  The schedule reproduces "18.2× retained ARR" within 1–2% across the case's churn range: the upfront covers the worst case, the earn-out pays only for revenue that survives control.
- Earn-out payable in Paytm shares (≤0.71 Cr shares, 1.1% of share count) inside a ±15% collar, or cash at Paytm's election if the stock has compressed and guardrails allow.

**WHY LIMINAL'S SHAREHOLDERS SAY YES (TA)** — ₹1,040 Cr of liquidity now; a defined path to the full ₹4,000 Cr headline; Paytm as anchor commercial client; founder and key-engineer retention pool vesting over the validation period; and the alternative buyer carries the same consumer-wallet conflict.

**TRANSACTION LOGIC** — Upfront consideration = strategic access. Contingent consideration = retained economic value.

## SLIDE 10 — CUSTOMER RETENTION & GOVERNANCE

### Protect the institutional franchise with structural independence that clients can verify, and pay sellers only for the clients who stay

**CONCENTRATION RISK (CF → CALC)** — HDFC Bank + Axis Bank + CoinDCX + ZebPay = 60% = ₹132 Cr of ₹220 Cr ARR. Case churn of ₹66–99 Cr equals 50–75% of the top-4 book. The revolt is a consumer-wallet conflict: banks and exchanges will not let a competing wallet operator see their custody flows.

**GOVERNANCE ARCHITECTURE — designed to be auditable by clients**
1. **Standalone brand and legal entity** — Liminal remains Liminal; no Paytm branding, no shared customer front-ends.
2. **Arm's-length board** — independent chair; Paytm 1 of 5 seats (2 of 7 on control); majority independent; reserved matters exclude day-to-day custody operations.
3. **Information barriers** — no Paytm access to client identities, positions or transaction data; MPC key shares never held in Paytm-controlled infrastructure; Paytm's own business served by a segregated desk on standard terms.
4. **Independent compliance, AML and cyber oversight** — Liminal's CCO/CISO report to the Liminal board's risk committee; annual independent attestation of the barriers (SOC 2-style) shared with clients.
5. **Neutral service standards** — a published client charter: no preferential pricing, routing or SLAs for Paytm-linked activity.
6. **Client-protection framework** — change-of-control consent rights, data-portability commitments, and structured pre-signing soundings with the top-4.
7. **Retention-linked consideration** — sellers' earn-out and the founder/management retention pool both scale with retained ARR; management's economics are aligned with keeping HDFC, Axis, CoinDCX and ZebPay.
8. **Founder and key-engineer retention** — Mahin Gupta and the MPC engineering core retained through the validation period on vesting economics.

**RETENTION ECONOMICS** — Under the earn-out, each 10 points of retained ARR is worth ₹291 Cr to sellers and ₹400 Cr of paid value to Paytm; the two sides are paid to protect the same clients.

**CORE MESSAGE** — Governance is not cosmetic; it is the mechanism that converts a 30–45% churn assumption into a ≤20% consent gate.

## SLIDE 11 — REGULATORY & CLOSING ARCHITECTURE

### Resolve the capital-treatment question before signing, and never take control until it is answered in writing

**BEFORE SIGNING (Phase 1)**
- RBI consultation on (a) a PA-licence holder taking a 26% stake in a digital-asset custody provider, (b) capital/reserve treatment at minority versus control. **Signing condition:** written comfort that no bank-like reserve applies at 26%.
- Diligence: AML programme and FIU-IND registration status, data protection and localisation of key material, cyber (ISO 27001 / SOC 2 evidence), litigation, client contracts (change-of-control clauses).
- Structured soundings with HDFC, Axis, CoinDCX, ZebPay under NDA on the ring-fence design.
- Competition and securities analysis: notifiability of Phase 1 and of the control tranche; Paytm listed-company disclosures.

**SIGNING → CLOSING (Phase 1)** — conditions precedent, as applicable: RBI comfort letter; no material adverse regulatory change; key-client consents where contracts require them at minority level; FIU-IND registration in good standing; cyber audit clean; stock-exchange disclosure.

**AT CONTROL EXERCISE (Phase 3)** — RBI change-in-control approval; competition clearance; SEBI/exchange approvals for any share-based earn-out (preferential-issue rules, shareholder resolution); client change-of-control consents (Gate 1); refreshed AML/cyber audit; no unresolved regulatory matter on either side.

**POST-CLOSE (all phases)** — standalone compliance framework; AML transaction monitoring and reporting; segregated data and key infrastructure; continuous cyber monitoring with incident reporting; independent compliance reporting to both boards' risk committees.

**THE ONE UNRESOLVED QUESTION THAT DECIDES THE DEAL** — Does minority ownership avoid, reduce or still trigger the ₹750 Cr requirement? The case does not say. Our model shows why it decides everything: even a ₹100 Cr/yr recurring cost pushes control FCF breakeven beyond ten years.

**HARD DECISION GATE** — No control without written regulatory capital treatment incorporated into the FCF model. If a reserve applies at minority level, Phase 1 is restructured toward a JV/licence before signing.

## SLIDE 12 — COMPETITIVE & DOWNSIDE RESPONSE

### Strategic urgency must not become a winner's curse: set the walk-away line now and let the structure absorb the downside

**PHONEPE–MASTERCARD THREAT (CF)** — an exploratory consortium to acquire Liminal if Paytm walks away. The case provides no competing price, terms, synergies or certainty of completion.

**WHAT HAPPENS IF THEY WIN** — PhonePe gains the institutional custody rail plus Mastercard's cross-border reach; Paytm's tokenized-settlement plan loses its neutral custodian and must build or partner elsewhere (24–36 months, TA). But the consortium inherits the same conflict — PhonePe runs a competing consumer wallet — so it faces the same revolt, the same 18× price for a breakeven business, and the same capital question. Mastercard's bank relationships may soften the revolt; that is the one real advantage they hold.

**PAYTM'S RESPONSE**
- **Reservation value:** total consideration ≤ ₹4,000 Cr, of which ≤ ₹1,040 Cr unconditional; no tranche above 18.2× retained ARR; no control at a recurring capital cost that defeats five-year FCF breakeven.
- **If outbid on unconditional terms: let them.** Winning at >18× for a business that loses 30–45% of revenue on change of control is the winner's curse the case describes.
- **Fallback:** secure a commercial custody/licensing partnership with an alternative institutional custodian and redeploy the ₹4,000 Cr toward buybacks and organic e₹ build.

**IF PAYTM'S MULTIPLE COMPRESSES** — Phase 1 is cash and does not depend on the stock. Mechanically a 25% price fall lifts the ₹1,900 Cr equity leg from 1.02 to 1.37 Cr shares (still ~2% dilution); the real risk is narrative — a −29% EPS print on a stock at ~247× earnings. Our answer: no unconditional equity, a ±15% collar on the earn-out shares, and cash settlement if guardrails allow.

**DOWNSIDE MATRIX — how the recommendation changes (answer to the Mission's "show how your recommendation changes")**

| | Capital drag: none / structured away | Pro-rata at 26% (≈₹195 Cr/yr) | Full ₹750 Cr at any ownership |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Client consents ≥80% (churn ≤20%)** | **Exercise** if bull trajectory (breakeven ≤5 yrs) and valuation cap hold | Hold; do not exercise; pursue JV/licence for capability | Do not exercise; hold 26% only if the minority itself is drag-free, else restructure/exit |
| **Consents 55–80% (churn 20–45%)** | **Hold** minority; earn-out floor protects; re-test at month 24 | Hold / restructure to JV | Restructure or exit |
| **Consents <55% (churn >45%)** | **No control**; remediation plan; consider exit via ROFO/tag | Exit | Exit |

**DOWNSIDE PLAYBOOK** — Churn >45% → no exercise, remediation, price protection · Capital unacceptable → remain minority / JV / exit · Key clients resist → strengthen ring-fence; no control without consents · Stock compresses → collar, cash settlement, no fixed-value issuance · PAT/FCF case fails → do not escalate capital on sunk cost · Regulatory/security conditions fail → suspend or terminate where rights permit.

**BOARD PRINCIPLE** — Competitive pressure may change timing; it must not change valuation discipline.

## SLIDE 13 — IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP & DECISION GATES

### Invest → Validate → Exercise / Hold / Walk Away — with quantified gates the Board can audit

**PHASE 1 · MONTHS 0–3 · INVEST**
Sign and close 26% for ₹1,040 Cr; commercial partnership live; standalone governance and information barriers installed; call option, earn-out schedule and ROFR/ROFO documented; RBI comfort on minority treatment obtained; founder/management retention pool in place; Board-approved transaction model with quarterly gate reporting.

**PHASE 2 · MONTHS 3–12 · VALIDATE**
- Customers: ARR retention, top-4 renewals, written consents to Paytm control under the ring-fence charter.
- Regulation: written capital treatment at control; approval path and timing; AML/FIU-IND standing.
- Financials: month-12 ARR (≥₹286 Cr signals the bull trajectory; ~₹264 Cr the base), margin progression, cash generation, partnership revenue, MDR conversion tracked separately.
- Risk: cyber, AML, data segregation, operational resilience, attestation of information barriers.

**PHASE 3 · MONTHS 12–24 · DECIDE**

| Gate | Threshold to exercise control |
|---|---|
| 1. Client consents | Clients representing ≥80% of ARR consent to Paytm control under the ring-fence; implied churn ≤20% |
| 2. Regulatory capital | Written RBI treatment; recurring capital cost compatible with Gate 5 (in practice: no bank-like reserve, or one structured away) |
| 3. Valuation | Control price ≤ ₹2,960 Cr and ≤ 18.2× retained ARR; total ≤ ₹4,000 Cr |
| 4. PAT | Consolidated PAT breakeven ≤5 years after exercise on the Board model (bull trajectory passes; base does not) |
| 5. FCF | Consolidated FCF breakeven ≤5 years after exercise, after confirmed capital cost; cash ≥ ₹5,000 Cr and gross debt/EBITDA ≤ 1.5× throughout |
| 6. Compliance | Clean AML/cyber/data audit; ISO 27001 and SOC 2 maintained; no unresolved regulatory matter |

**EXERCISE** all six clear. **HOLD** strategic value intact but any gate open — Liminal can remain an arm's-length associate indefinitely. **WALK AWAY / UNWIND** churn >45%, capital drag defeats returns, key clients reject ownership, regulatory deterioration, or price above reservation value.

**FINAL BOARD MESSAGE** — Paytm should not pay today for risks it can observe tomorrow. Secure Liminal's infrastructure through a 26% stake and a commercial partnership. Preserve the right, not the obligation, to acquire control. Commit the remaining ₹2,960 Cr only after client trust, regulatory capital treatment, valuation and cash-flow economics are proven. **Strategic access now. Control only when value is protected.**
