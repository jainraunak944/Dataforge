TITLE SLIDE — PROJECT SATOSHI GATE — Paytm × Liminal Custody Solutions — Board Transaction Recommendation — "Strategic access today. Control only when the economics are proven."

SLIDE 1 — BOARD RECOMMENDATION — Secure the capability, not the full-control risk. Do not acquire 100% of Liminal today. Pursue a 20–30% strategic minority investment, anchored at 25% as a midpoint team assumption, combined with: commercial custody/MPC partnership; standalone governance; pre-agreed call option for future control; retention- and performance-linked future consideration. Why: strategic fit is real; full control creates value leakage (30–45% ARR churn); regulatory economics severe (₹750 Cr recurring FCF drag); near-term earnings support limited (Liminal ~PAT breakeven); staging preserves optionality. Board ask: Invest → Validate → Exercise / Hold / Walk Away; gates: Client Retention | Regulatory Capital | Valuation | PAT | FCF | Compliance.

SLIDE 2 — STRATEGIC RATIONALE — Paytm today (UPI, merchant payments, consumer financial services) + Liminal adds (institutional custody, MPC, secure asset infrastructure) → bridge: Payments → Custody & MPC → Tokenized assets/treasury → e₹/CBDC → B2B & cross-border settlement. Key question: does Paytm need 100% today? Our view: no, strategic access can precede strategic control.

SLIDE 3 — THE OWNERSHIP PARADOX — ₹4,000 Cr full acquisition creates four pressures: customer conflict (30–45% ARR lost); regulatory capital (₹750 Cr recurring FCF drag); financial pressure (Liminal ~PAT breakeven); valuation pressure (₹4,000 Cr ÷ ₹220 Cr = 18.2× headline ARR before churn). Core paradox: Paytm wants Liminal for its institutional franchise, but taking control may weaken that franchise. Separate strategic attractiveness from economic attractiveness of owning 100% today.

SLIDE 4 — STRATEGIC OPTIONS — Table: Full acquisition / Minority / JV / Walk away across strategic access, control, churn exposure ("not specified — validate" for minority and JV), ₹750 Cr treatment ("not specified — validate"), upfront commitment, reversibility (Low / High / Medium / High), future-control optionality. Conclusion: minority + partnership + call option defers the two largest unresolved risks. Discipline: do not assume minority eliminates churn or the ₹750 Cr requirement; these become validation gates.

SLIDE 5 — RECOMMENDED TRANSACTION ARCHITECTURE — Phase 1: 25% stake, if priced pro-rata to ₹4,000 Cr → ₹1,000 Cr from existing cash; no new debt; no new equity; gross cash ~₹7,900 Cr. + commercial partnership + governance ring-fence + pre-agreed call option. Future-control pricing: lower of headline mechanics or retained-ARR/performance-based value, subject to retention, capital treatment, performance, valuation cap, customary adjustments. Pay for access today; pay for control only after retained value is observable.

SLIDE 6 — CASE FACTS VS TEAM ASSUMPTIONS — Case facts listed (Paytm and Liminal/deal). Team assumptions listed by NAME only: initial ownership 25%, ₹1,000 Cr, existing cash, validation period, minority/JV churn, minority/JV capital treatment, ARR growth, margins, debt cost and tax, WACC, integration costs, synergies, MDR conversion, option mechanics, future-control valuation methodology. Principle: case fact ≠ derived calculation ≠ team assumption.

SLIDE 7 — VALUATION & CHURN — 18.2× headline → 26.0× / 29.1× / 33.1× at 30 / 37.5 / 45% churn; implied value at 18.2× on retained ARR ~₹2,800 / ₹2,500 / ₹2,200 Cr; recovery hurdle +42.9% (30% churn) and +81.8% (45%); structuring response: deferred consideration, earn-out, CVR, retention-linked payout.

SLIDE 8 — FINANCING & EPS IMPACT — Full-acquisition counterfactual: ₹2,100 Cr cash from balance sheet → cash ₹6,800 Cr; ₹1,900 Cr equity at ~₹1,855/share ≈ 1.02 Cr new shares → 65.02 Cr; if PAT stays ₹480 Cr → EPS ≈ ₹7.38 vs ₹7.50 = ≈1.6% mechanical dilution before financing costs, acquired earnings, synergies, integration costs. Phase 1: ₹1,000 Cr cash, no debt, no shares, no dilution. Future-control financing: do not pre-commit the mix; preserve liquidity, rating headroom, cap leverage, avoid excessive dilution, use contingent consideration.

SLIDE 9 — PAT & FCF BREAKEVEN — Formula slides: incremental PAT = Liminal PAT + synergies − financing − integration; incremental FCF = Liminal FCF + cash synergies + applicable MDR − ₹750 Cr drag − integration cash costs. MDR ₹115 Cr is not automatically PAT/FCF and not created by the deal; model separately. Case does not give Liminal FCF/margins/capex/WC/growth → breakeven year must be assumption-driven. Rule: no control until credible PAT/EPS and FCF breakeven after capital requirements.

SLIDE 10 — CUSTOMER RETENTION & GOVERNANCE — HDFC + Axis + CoinDCX + ZebPay = 60% = ₹132 Cr of ₹220 Cr; churn ₹66–99 Cr = ~50–75% of top-4 ARR. Governance: standalone brand; arm's-length board with independent directors and reserved matters; information barriers; independent compliance & cyber oversight; neutral service standards; client-protection framework; retention-linked consideration. "Governance is a valuation-protection mechanism."

SLIDE 11 — REGULATORY & CLOSING ARCHITECTURE — Before signing / signing-to-closing / post-close checklists (RBI, competition, securities, client consents, infosec, no MAC; standalone compliance, AML, data segregation, cyber monitoring, independent reporting). Most important unresolved question: does minority ownership avoid, reduce or still trigger the ₹750 Cr requirement? Hard gate: no full control until capital treatment is known.

SLIDE 12 — COMPETITIVE & DOWNSIDE RESPONSE — PhonePe–Mastercard consortium creates opportunity cost but the case gives no competing valuation/terms/synergies/certainty; do not automatically match; set a risk-adjusted reservation value; walk away above it. Downside playbook: churn >45% → no control / price protection; capital unacceptable → remain minority / restructure / exit; key clients resist → strengthen ring-fence; share price compresses → avoid fixed-value equity; PAT/FCF fails → do not escalate; regulatory/security fails → suspend/terminate.

SLIDE 13 — IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP & DECISION GATES — Phase 1 (0–3 months) invest; Phase 2 (3–12 months) validate customers, regulation, financials, risk; Phase 3 (~12 months+) exercise / hold / walk away, with qualitative conditions. Final message: "Paytm should not pay today for risks it can observe tomorrow."
