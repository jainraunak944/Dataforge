## SLIDE 1 — BOARD RECOMMENDATION

### Buy a 26% stake and a call option for ₹1,040 Cr now; do not buy 100% of Liminal for ₹4,000 Cr today

**RECOMMENDATION — a staged structure, not a full acquisition**

1. **Invest ₹1,040 Cr in cash for 26% of Liminal** (0.9% of market cap, 12% of cash). 26% is one share above the 25% special-resolution threshold: negative control without the operational control that triggers the client revolt and the capital requirement.
2. **Sign a commercial custody/MPC partnership** for Paytm's tokenized-treasury, e₹ and cross-border settlement use cases.
3. **Ring-fence Liminal**: standalone brand, arm's-length board, information barriers, independent compliance.
4. **Hold a call option on the remaining 74%**, exercisable months 12–24, capped at ₹2,960 Cr and paid as ₹1,650 Cr upfront + up to ₹1,310 Cr retention earn-out. Maximum total ever payable: ₹4,000 Cr. Maximum unconditional: ₹1,040 Cr.

**WHY NOT 100% TODAY — four numbers the Board should hold**

| Pressure | Case fact → calculation | So what |
|---|---|---|
| Client revolt | 30–45% of ARR lost within 12 months of full acquisition (CF) | Effective price rises from 18.2× to 26–33× retained ARR (CALC) |
| Capital trap | ₹750 Cr/yr recurring FCF drag at full ownership (CF) | Larger than Paytm's entire EBITDA (₹640 Cr, CF); 10-year NPV ₹4,238 Cr exceeds the ₹4,000 Cr price (TA: 12% WACC) |
| Earnings | Liminal ~breakeven; EPS impact comes from new shares and financing (CF) | Year-1 EPS ₹5.29 vs ₹7.50 (−29%) at the headline mix once foregone interest and the post-churn loss are counted (TA); ₹6.08 (−19%) even at zero churn |
| Breakeven | ₹750 Cr drag vs ₹115 Cr MDR (CF) | Full-acquisition FCF never breaks even within 10 years in any scenario; MDR offsets at most 15% of the drag (CALC) |

**BASE EXPECTATION, STATED PLAINLY** — Under the case's own assumptions the modal outcome is **Hold**: the ₹750 Cr treatment makes control uneconomic. The option is bought as insurance and as a block on the PhonePe–Mastercard consortium, not as a plan to buy. Control is exercised only if Liminal earns it (Slide 13).

**BOARD ASK** — Approve **Invest (₹1,040 Cr) → Validate (12 months) → Exercise / Hold / Walk Away**. Authorise: (i) the ₹1,040 Cr investment and shareholder agreement; (ii) negotiation of the option, earn-out and ROFR/ROFO; (iii) the guardrails (cash ≥ ₹5,000 Cr, gross debt/EBITDA ≤ 1.5×, no unconditional equity); (iv) delegation of gate reviews to the Board's M&A/Risk committee. Control only after six gates clear: **Client consents · Regulatory capital · Valuation cap · PAT breakeven ≤ 5 yrs · FCF breakeven ≤ 5 yrs · Compliance**.

## SLIDE 2 — STRATEGIC RATIONALE

### Liminal extends Paytm from payments into institutional digital-settlement infrastructure; access captures most of that value, ownership adds the risk

**PAYTM TODAY (CF)** — UPI, merchant payments, consumer financial services. ₹1,18,750 Cr market cap on a turnaround re-rating; UPI and checkout growth slowing; PhonePe and Google Pay dominate volume; regulatory scrutiny since RBI's 2024 Paytm Payments Bank restrictions; stated ambition in tokenized treasury and cross-border settlement aligned with RBI's e₹ pilot.

**LIMINAL ADDS (CF)** — Institutional digital-asset custody and MPC infrastructure (founded 2021 by Mahin Gupta; ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II). Clients: exchanges, hedge funds, OTC desks. HDFC Bank, Axis Bank, CoinDCX, ZebPay = 60% of ₹220 Cr ARR.

**STRATEGIC BRIDGE**
Payments → Institutional custody & MPC → Tokenized assets / treasury → e₹ / CBDC settlement rails → B2B & cross-border settlement

**WHY IT MATTERS**
- Custody/MPC is the trust layer every tokenized-settlement product needs; certifications and institutional trust take years to build in-house (TA).
- It is adjacent to the core, not a replacement: it gives the B2B settlement ambition a regulated-grade foundation.
- It is a hedge on the e₹/tokenization thesis: if it scales, Paytm holds the rail; if not, the cheque is ₹1,040 Cr, not ₹4,000 Cr.

**IS THE OPPORTUNITY LARGE ENOUGH TO LEAVE THE CORE?** — Large enough for a 26% position and a partnership; not large enough, on the case's numbers, to justify a ₹4,000 Cr control cheque with a ₹750 Cr/yr carrying cost. We size the bet to the evidence.

**COULD A COMMERCIAL OR LICENSING PARTNERSHIP DO THE SAME JOB?** — For the *capability*, largely yes. For *security of supply*, no: a pure partnership leaves Liminal for sale, and the case says the consortium buys it if Paytm walks. Then Paytm's critical custody supplier is owned by its main competitor. The 26% stake with ROFR/ROFO and a call option buys the blocking right and the information rights to run the validation; the partnership captures the capability. Together they cost 26% of the headline price.

**WHAT WALKING AWAY COSTS** — No neutral custodian for the settlement plan; a 24–36 month build-or-partner path (TA); the consortium owns the rail. That, not fear of the counter-bid, is why we act now, and why we act small.

**THE HONEST TRADE-OFF** — On a pure earnings basis no structure is accretive before year 5. This is a strategic-positioning investment; the cheque is sized accordingly.

## SLIDE 3 — THE OWNERSHIP PARADOX

### Full ownership impairs the asset Paytm is paying for, and the capital drag alone is worth more than the company

**₹4,000 Cr FULL ACQUISITION CREATES FOUR IMMEDIATE PRESSURES**

**1. Customer conflict (CF → CALC)** — Top-4 clients = ₹132 Cr of ₹220 Cr ARR. The case assumes ₹66–99 Cr of ARR (30–45%) leaves within 12 months, i.e. 50–75% of the top-4 book. The trigger is Paytm's consumer wallet: banks and exchanges will not let a competing wallet operator own their custodian.

**2. Regulatory capital (CF → CALC)** — ₹750 Cr/yr recurring FCF drag = 117% of Paytm EBITDA, 156% of PAT, 3.4× Liminal's entire revenue. To self-fund the drag Liminal would need ~₹4,000 Cr ARR (18× today) at a 25% EBITDA margin: 16 years away at 20% growth, 11 at 30% (TA).

**3. Earnings (CF → TA)** — Liminal is ~PAT breakeven, so the deal brings no earnings to offset its own cost. Year-1 EPS at the case's ₹2,100 Cr cash + ₹1,900 Cr equity mix: ₹5.29 (−29%) after foregone treasury income (₹94.5 Cr) and a ₹41 Cr post-churn Liminal loss. The "share-count only" view (₹7.38, −1.6%) hides 95% of the dilution.

**4. Valuation (CALC)** — ₹4,000 Cr ÷ ₹220 Cr = 18.2× headline ARR → 26.0× / 29.1× / 33.1× retained ARR at 30 / 37.5 / 45% churn. Every 10 points of churn destroys ₹400 Cr of value at the paid multiple. Paytm itself trades at ~247× trailing earnings: a −29% EPS print invites exactly the buyback-versus-M&A pressure the case warns about.

**CORE DEAL PARADOX** — Paytm wants Liminal for its institutional franchise. Taking control is what breaks that franchise and what triggers the capital requirement.

**BOARD IMPLICATION** — Separate the *strategic attractiveness of Liminal* (high) from the *economic attractiveness of owning 100% today* (negative under the case's own assumptions).

## SLIDE 5 — RECOMMENDED TRANSACTION ARCHITECTURE

### Pay ₹1,040 Cr for access and an option today; pay for control only against retained ARR, and never more than ₹4,000 Cr in total

**PHASE 1 — STRATEGIC ENTRY (TA)**
- **26% for ₹1,040 Cr**: secondary purchase from existing shareholders at pro-rata of the ₹4,000 Cr headline. This is the modelled ceiling. Fundamental value of 26% is ₹456 Cr (base DCF) to ₹951 Cr (bull); the ceiling therefore carries up to ₹584 Cr of strategic and option premium, which is the price of blocking the consortium and securing the rail. Negotiating target: ₹850–950 Cr (bull-DCF pro-rata).
- Funded 100% from balance-sheet cash: no new debt, no new shares. Cash after: ₹7,860 Cr. Accounted as an equity-method associate (no consolidation, no goodwill line).
- **Why 26%, not 25%**: one share above 25% blocks special resolutions (preferential allotment to a new investor, charter changes, schemes of arrangement). The veto does not stop a secondary sale by other holders; the ROFR/ROFO does. Together with the call option they close every route to the consortium.
- **Rights**: 1 of 5 board seats plus an observer; information and audit rights; ROFR/ROFO on all transfers; tag-along; anti-dilution; reserved matters (new investor, sale of business, change of custody model, related-party dealings with Paytm).

**+ COMMERCIAL PARTNERSHIP** — Multi-year custody/MPC services agreement for Paytm's tokenized-treasury, e₹ and cross-border pilots, at arm's length, served by a segregated desk (Slide 10).

**+ GOVERNANCE RING-FENCE** — Standalone brand, independent chair, information barriers, independent compliance and cyber reporting, published client charter (Slide 10).

**+ CALL OPTION ON THE REMAINING 74% (TA)**
- Window months 12–24 after Phase 1 close; a right, not an obligation. After month 24 the option lapses; ROFR/ROFO persist.
- **Price cap ₹2,960 Cr** (74% of headline) = **₹1,650 Cr at exercise + retention earn-out up to ₹1,310 Cr**, linear between 55% and 100% retained ARR, measured at month 18 post-control on the pre-control client base, audited, excluding any Paytm-related revenue (so Paytm's own business cannot inflate it).

- Earn-out settled in Paytm shares (≤ 0.71 Cr shares, 1.1% of the count) inside a ±15% collar, or in cash at Paytm's election if the stock has compressed and the guardrails allow.

If the option is refused: fall back to 26% + ROFR/ROFO without the call, which still blocks and still gives access.

**TRANSACTION LOGIC** — Upfront consideration = strategic access. Contingent consideration = retained economic value.

- MDR covers **15.3% of the drag at the revenue line** and **9.2% (₹69 Cr) after conversion to PAT/FCF**. Net cash cost of full ownership after the MDR: ~₹681 Cr/yr.
- The MDR accrues to Paytm whether or not it buys Liminal: a standalone tailwind (≈ +₹1.08 EPS), not a deal synergy. A breakeven that relies on it is a breakeven of Paytm, not of the transaction.
- Liminal would need ~₹4,000 Cr ARR (18× today) to cover the drag from its own cash flow.

**HOW MUCH RECURRING CAPITAL COST CAN CONTROL BEAR?** — Even ₹100 Cr/yr fails the five-year FCF gate in every trajectory; ₹50 Cr/yr passes only in bull with zero churn. Control is therefore viable only if the reserve is nil or structured away at the custodian level. At the case's ₹750 Cr it is never exercised.

**CONTROL-EXERCISE RULE** — Exercise only when the Board model shows consolidated PAT breakeven **and** FCF breakeven within five years of exercise after the confirmed capital treatment. Otherwise Paytm holds the 26% and the partnership.

## SLIDE 10 — CUSTOMER RETENTION & GOVERNANCE

### Protect the institutional franchise with independence that clients can verify, and pay sellers only for the clients who stay

**CONCENTRATION RISK (CF → CALC)** — HDFC Bank + Axis Bank + CoinDCX + ZebPay = 60% = ₹132 Cr of ₹220 Cr ARR. Case churn of ₹66–99 Cr equals 50–75% of the top-4 book. The revolt is a consumer-wallet conflict: banks and exchanges will not let a competing wallet operator see their custody flows.

**GOVERNANCE ARCHITECTURE — designed to be audited by clients, not just described to them**
1. **Standalone brand and legal entity** — Liminal stays Liminal: no Paytm branding, no shared customer front-ends.
2. **Arm's-length board** — independent chair; Paytm 1 of 5 seats (2 of 7 on control); independent majority; reserved matters exclude day-to-day custody operations.
3. **Information barriers** — no Paytm access to client identities, positions or transaction data; MPC key shares never held in Paytm-controlled infrastructure; Paytm's own business served by a segregated desk on standard terms.
4. **Independent compliance, AML and cyber oversight** — Liminal's CCO and CISO report to the Liminal board's risk committee; annual independent attestation of the barriers (SOC 2-style) shared with clients.
5. **Neutral service standards** — a published client charter: no preferential pricing, routing or SLAs for Paytm-linked activity.
6. **Client-protection framework** — change-of-control consent rights, data-portability commitments, pre-signing soundings with the top-4 under NDA, and a named relationship owner per top-4 client through the validation period.
7. **Retention-linked consideration** — the sellers' earn-out and the founder/management retention pool both scale with retained ARR; each 10 points of retained ARR moves the earn-out by ₹291 Cr, roughly 74% of the ₹400 Cr those clients are worth at the headline multiple. Both sides are paid to protect the same clients.
8. **Founder and key-engineer retention** — Mahin Gupta and the MPC engineering core retained through the validation period on vesting economics; independent-director nomination rights for the founder while the ring-fence stands.

**HOW SIGNIFICANT IS THE RISK OF LOSING THE TOP-4?** — Under full control, existential: the case's own range removes half to three-quarters of their revenue. Under the ring-fenced minority, manageable: no change of control occurs, contracts are unaffected, and the consent process tells the Board in advance what control would cost.

**CORE MESSAGE** — Governance is not cosmetic. It is the mechanism that converts a 30–45% churn assumption into a ≤ 20% consent gate, and it is what the earn-out pays sellers to protect.

## SLIDE 11 — REGULATORY & CLOSING ARCHITECTURE

### Resolve the capital-treatment question before signing, and never take control until it is answered in writing

**BEFORE SIGNING (Phase 1; indicative 8–12 weeks, TA)**
- RBI consultation on (a) a PA-licence holder taking a 26% stake in a digital-asset custody provider and (b) capital/reserve treatment at minority versus control. **Signing condition: written comfort that no bank-like reserve applies at 26%.**
- Diligence: AML programme and FIU-IND registration standing; data-protection and localisation of key material; cyber evidence (ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II); litigation; client contracts (change-of-control clauses and their thresholds).
- Structured top-4 soundings under NDA on the ring-fence design and on their conditions for future consent.
- Competition and securities analysis: notifiability of Phase 1 and of the control tranche; Paytm's listed-company disclosures.

**SIGNING → CLOSING (Phase 1)** — Conditions precedent, as applicable: RBI comfort letter; no material adverse regulatory change on either side; key-client consents where contracts bite at minority level; FIU-IND registration in good standing; clean cyber audit; stock-exchange disclosure on signing.

**AT CONTROL EXERCISE (Phase 3; indicative 4–6 months, TA)** — RBI change-in-control approval; competition clearance; SEBI/exchange approvals for any share-settled earn-out (preferential-issue rules, shareholder resolution); client change-of-control consents (Gate 1); refreshed AML/cyber audit; no unresolved regulatory matter on either side.

**POST-CLOSE (all phases)** — Standalone compliance framework; AML transaction monitoring and reporting; segregated data and key infrastructure; continuous cyber monitoring and incident reporting; independent compliance reporting to both boards' risk committees.

**DOES OWNING CUSTODY CREATE ADDITIONAL REQUIREMENTS?** — Yes, per the case: bank-like reserves against custodied assets at full ownership (₹750 Cr/yr). Our design keeps the custody licence, the custodied assets and the reserves, if any, inside Liminal, and keeps Paytm at negative control until the treatment is written down.

**THE ONE UNRESOLVED QUESTION THAT DECIDES THE DEAL** — Does minority ownership avoid, reduce or still trigger the ₹750 Cr requirement? The case does not say. Slide 9 shows why it decides everything: even a ₹100 Cr/yr recurring cost defeats the five-year FCF gate in every trajectory.

**HARD DECISION GATE** — No control without written regulatory capital treatment incorporated into the FCF model. If a reserve applies at minority level, Phase 1 is restructured toward the JV/licence path before signing.

## SLIDE 12 — COMPETITIVE & DOWNSIDE RESPONSE

### Strategic urgency must not become a winner's curse: set the walk-away line now and let the structure absorb the downside

**PHONEPE–MASTERCARD THREAT (CF)** — An exploratory consortium to acquire Liminal if Paytm walks away. The case provides no competing price, terms, synergies or certainty of completion.

**IF THEY WIN** — PhonePe gains the institutional custody rail and Mastercard's cross-border reach; Paytm's settlement plan loses its neutral custodian and must build or partner elsewhere (24–36 months, TA). But the consortium inherits the same conflict: PhonePe runs a competing consumer wallet, so it faces the same revolt, the same 18× price for a breakeven business and the same capital question. Mastercard's bank relationships may soften the revolt; that is the one real advantage they hold, and it is not worth overpaying to neutralise.

**PAYTM'S RESPONSE**
- **Reservation value**: total consideration ≤ ₹4,000 Cr, of which ≤ ₹1,040 Cr unconditional; no tranche above 18.2× retained ARR; no control at a recurring capital cost that defeats five-year FCF breakeven.
- **If outbid on unconditional terms: let them.** Winning at >18× for a business that loses 30–45% of revenue on change of control is the winner's curse the case describes.
- **Fallback**: a commercial custody/licensing partnership with an alternative institutional custodian for the e₹ pilots, and redeployment of the ₹4,000 Cr toward buybacks and organic B2B settlement build.

**IF PAYTM'S MULTIPLE COMPRESSES** — Phase 1 is cash and does not depend on the stock. Mechanically a 25% price fall lifts the ₹1,900 Cr equity leg from 1.02 to 1.37 Cr shares (still ~2% dilution); the real risk is narrative: a −29% EPS print on a stock at ~247× earnings. Our answer: no unconditional equity, a ±15% collar on the earn-out shares, cash settlement if guardrails allow, and no fixed-value issuance. The transaction is justifiable to shareholders because the unconditional cheque is 0.9% of market cap.

**DOWNSIDE MATRIX — how the recommendation changes**

| | Capital drag: none / structured away | Pro-rata at 26% (≈ ₹195 Cr/yr) | Full ₹750 Cr at any ownership |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Client consents ≥ 80% (churn ≤ 20%)** | **Exercise** if bull trajectory (breakeven ≤ 5 yrs) and valuation cap hold | Hold; do not exercise; JV/licence for capability | Do not exercise; hold 26% only if the minority itself is drag-free, else restructure or exit |
| **Consents 55–80% (churn 20–45%)** | **Hold** the minority; earn-out floor protects; re-test at month 24 | Hold or restructure to JV | Restructure or exit |
| **Consents < 55% (churn > 45%)** | **No control**; remediation ladder below | Exit | Exit |

**REMEDIATION AND UNWIND LADDER IF CHURN OR CONSENTS BREACH 45%**
1. Day 30: independent review of the cause; CEO-level outreach to each top-4 client; Paytm director steps back to observer.
2. Day 90: governance reset, published attestation of the barriers, commercial remedies (fee holidays, SLA guarantees) funded from the sellers' earn-out at risk.
3. Day 180: if retention is still below 55%, no exercise; run the ROFO/tag process to sell the stake to a neutral strategic or financial buyer, or convert to licence + warrants. Sunk cost never justifies escalation.

**BOARD PRINCIPLE** — Competitive pressure may change timing; it must not change valuation discipline.
