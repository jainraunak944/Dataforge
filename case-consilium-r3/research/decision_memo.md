# Decision memo (private): which strategy, and what would reverse it

## The commercial problem in one paragraph
ATP interest on FanCode is spiky (India-friendly Masters weeks index ~150 vs 100 in ordinary weeks; marquee matches
often start around 1 AM IST) while the first purchase most entrants make, a ₹79-₹99 tournament pass, cannot repay a
₹150-₹200 acquisition cost on its own. Ordinary-week viewers are ~90% existing fans, so ordinary weeks are a
retention and upgrade audience, not a mass-acquisition audience. Performance spend (65-70% of budget, peaking at
T-48h) converts, but part of it converts people who would have paid anyway, and F1/football organic growth confounds
attribution. The question is therefore not "how do we buy more ATP viewers" but "which entrants become worth
₹150-₹200, through what second and third paid decisions, and how do we prove it".

## Options compared
| Option | What it is | Economics (model) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Broad sports acquisition | Push tournament passes to the wide registered base and adjacent-sport viewers with more performance spend | Cold prospecting at assumed CPM ₹120 / CTR 0.7% / CVR 1.5% gives attributed CAC well above ₹1,000; tournament entrants are worth ~₹130 in 12 months, so even at ₹183 CAC they do not pay back inside a year | Rejected as the lead; kept as a ₹1 lakh measured learning test |
| B. Lower-price entry offers | Cheap session passes and steep discounts to drive first purchase | Session entrants are worth ~₹70 in 12 months; a ₹19-₹39 session only makes sense where CAC is near zero (owned channels) and where a credit ladder pulls the buyer up to tournament and season-level spend; cannibalisation must be capped | Adopted only inside the ladder, never as a paid-media hook |
| C. Selective acquisition + deeper use of existing demand (chosen) | Acquire where intent is observable (tennis-page visitors, free-content tennis viewers, lapsed 2025 buyers, adjacent-sport pass holders), fit the product to the viewing schedule (morning replay), engineer the second occasion (Race to Turin), and move buyers up a public credit ladder to a finish-the-season pass; put paid-media weight behind the ₹199 finish-the-season entry in marquee weeks | Finish-the-season entrants are worth ~₹264 in 12 months and ~₹410 within the rights horizon; owned-channel entrants cost ₹60-₹135; search/retargeting at ~₹183 attributed meets the target; upgrade share is the lever (each +10pp is worth ~₹12 per tournament entrant in 12 months, ~₹19 in 24) | Selected |

## Working hypothesis after the model
FanCode can turn ATP peaks into durable value by (1) spending acquisition money only on observable intent and on the
finish-the-season entry, (2) making overnight tennis watchable the next morning without spoilers, and (3) earning the
second paid decision (upgrade credit within 30 days) before paying to acquire another low-intent viewer. Tournament-pass
entrants cannot repay ₹150-₹200 within 12 months at base assumptions; they can within the 2028 rights horizon if the
upgrade share reaches ~27% (base 20%, ladder target 30%).

## Deliberate trade-offs
1. Defer broad low-intent acquisition (cricket-only users, generic sports audiences) until the pilot shows measured lift.
2. Cap paid conversion spend on sessions that start after 23:00 IST at 15% of performance spend; sell those sessions as a morning-replay occasion instead.
3. Convert ESPN.in takeovers from always-on to a geo/time-split test with a ₹1 lakh cap until they demonstrate incremental value.
4. Reduce the performance share from 65-70% to 52% in the pilot, funding measurement, retention and editorial; the reallocation rule returns money to performance if attributed CAC stays ≤ ₹200 and incremental CAC ≤ ₹300.

## What would reverse the choice
- Measured CRM lift below 30% relative (owned channels do not move purchase behaviour): shift back to paid retargeting and drop the ladder incentives.
- Upgrade share below 12% by day 45 despite the credit: the ladder is not pulling; replace with direct finish-the-season promotion only.
- Cold prospecting attributed CAC below ₹300 with FTS mix: scale prospecting rather than owned channels.
- Morning replay opens below 15% of pushes with no lift in next-event return: cut the editorial cost line.
