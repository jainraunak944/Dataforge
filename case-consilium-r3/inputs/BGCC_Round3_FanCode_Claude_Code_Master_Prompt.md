### Unit economics

Distinguish:

- Cash collected versus revenue recognized over an access period.
- Taxes versus revenue.
- Incremental payment, streaming, support, refund, and revenue-share costs.
- Acquisition and retention marketing costs, counted once.
- Existing fixed rights commitments versus incremental rights obligations and allocated full-cost economics.

Use symbolic tax/cost inputs until verified or explicitly assumed. If a customer price includes tax at rate `t`, net sales before other deductions are `cash_collected / (1 + t)`. Do not subtract the same tax or credit twice. Do not assume an actual confidential ATP rights fee or claim full profitability while excluding material fixed costs.

Calculate contribution before marketing for each product and customer pathway. Then subtract the applicable marketing spend. Include at least:

1. A new low-commitment buyer who never returns.
2. A repeat tournament purchaser who does not upgrade.
3. A purchaser who upgrades using a credit.
4. A direct season purchaser.
5. Any proposed incremental cross-sport upgrade, with rights and cannibalization implications.

Branches must be mutually exclusive or tracked explicitly through time. Repeat purchases and season upgrades cannot both be counted as if every customer makes every purchase. Existing subscribers are not new acquisition revenue. A rupee of bundle revenue may appear only once in the platform total.

Use 90-day and 12-month contribution views where decision-useful. Model longer horizons only within a justified rights/contract horizon, with disclosed renewal assumptions. Treat the case's 60–65% repeat observation and 90% renewal observation as informative baselines with limitations, not universal constants.

For a two-path illustration, if a share `u` upgrades and the remaining share stays on the entry path:

```text
Expected contribution before acquisition =
    (1 - u) * C_entry_path + u * C_upgrade_path

Break-even upgrade share =
    (CAC - C_entry_path) / (C_upgrade_path - C_entry_path)
```

Use this only when the branch contributions cover the same horizon and already include all relevant entry/upgrade receipts and costs. A result above 100% signals that the strategy cannot break even under those assumptions. A zero/negative denominator requires separate interpretation.

Build downside, base, and upside cases. Vary the assumptions that drive the result: repeat purchases, upgrade share, incremental acquisition, net price/mix, variable costs, and cannibalization. Include one compact sensitivity table and a genuine break-even threshold. Do not tune assumptions until the target looks met.

### CAC arithmetic safeguards

The basic financial tension must be visible: **₹79–₹99 in initial tournament receipts cannot cover ₹150–₹200 acquisition cost on that purchase alone, even before service costs.** Show which cohorts justify acquisition through later contribution, and which do not.

For contests, the case provides `purchase_CVR = 0.078`. Before other costs and assuming every purchase is a new payer, a ₹150–₹200 CAC implies a media CPC ceiling of:

```text
150 * 0.078 = ₹11.70
200 * 0.078 = ₹15.60
```

This is a derived screening range, not an observed CPC. If `n` is the share of purchases from new payers and `f` is the incremental share of those new payers, then:

```text
Media-only incremental CAC = CPC / (0.078 * n * f)
```

Add production, partner fees, prizes, fulfillment, and other applicable costs for a fully loaded measure. If conversion is already defined as incremental new-payer conversion, do not apply `n` and `f` again.

Calculate blended CAC from total relevant spend divided by total deduplicated eligible acquired payers. For comparable channels only, with spend shares `w_i` and consistent `CAC_i`:

```text
Blended CAC = 1 / sum(w_i / CAC_i)
```

It is **not** `sum(w_i * CAC_i)`. Do not use the harmonic formula for rows that have incompatible denominators, overlapping conversions, or no direct acquisition outcome. The cohort/budget model is authoritative.

### Measurement design

The case explicitly questions conversions that would have happened anyway. Include an executable incrementality test:

- Define an eligible audience and persistent randomized holdout where feasible.
- Stratify or otherwise balance existing payment status, sports affinity, tournament tier, and viewing window.
- Prevent a control user from receiving the same campaign through another owned channel. Report unavoidable paid-media spillover.
- For broad publisher activity where user randomization is unavailable, consider a credible geo/time design and acknowledge event-strength confounding.
- Separate campaign attribution from causal impact. Last-click tracking alone is insufficient.
- Do not count a paid customer already entitled to ATP as an incremental acquisition.

For a simple randomized test:

```text
Treatment conversion = new payers in treatment / eligible treatment users
Control conversion = new payers in control / eligible control users
Incremental new payers in treatment =
    (treatment conversion - control conversion) * eligible treatment users

Incremental CAC = incremental campaign cost / incremental new payers
```

Use intention-to-treat denominators. Scale to the whole population only when justified. If measured lift is zero or negative, report incremental CAC as undefined/unfavourable, not a positive success metric.

Define observation windows, baseline needs, minimum detectable effect or practical sample-size requirements, and the owner of the analysis. Do not promise statistical certainty from an unspecified or inadequate sample. Retention readouts must wait for cohorts to mature.
