The commercial problem
ATP demand on FanCode is spiky: India-friendly Masters weeks index about 150 against 100 in 
ordinary weeks, marquee European evenings start at 00:00-01:00 IST, and about 90% of 
ordinary-week viewers are already fans. A ₹79-₹99 first purchase leaves roughly ₹62 of 
contribution before marketing, so it cannot repay ₹150-₹200 on its own, and organic F1/football 
growth confounds what performance spend is credited with. Trade-offs made: defer broad 
acquisition, cap overnight live-conversion spend, turn takeovers into a measured test.
Cohorts first
1. Observable tennis intent that does not pay yet (free tennis viewers, tennis-page visitors, 
lapsed 2025 buyers): the only new-payer cohort worth paid media, and only at T-48h. 2. 
Finish-the-Season buyers in marquee weeks (Shanghai, Paris, ATP Finals). 3. F1, MotoGP and 
LaLiga pass holders, cross-sold a session that fits their hours and reported separately. ATP 
core fans are a retention and upgrade audience; broad casual audiences are deferred.
Connected actions
A public credit ladder (session ₹19-₹39, tournament ₹79-₹99, Finish-the-Season ₹199), each 
rung credited to the next within 30 days; a morning-replay journey for overnight sessions 
(spoiler-free push at 07:00 IST with a 20-minute cut); a Race-to-Turin narrative that gives 
ordinary weeks a reason; and a season-end bridge into the ₹179 all-sport Monthly Pass and 
the 2027 season at a ₹349 loyalty price.

Bridge 1: ATP to the rest of the portfolio
Verified access rule: an ATP tournament or Finish-the-Season 
pass gives no F1, MotoGP or LaLiga access. The Monthly Pass 
(₹179, S10) covers all paywalled sports for a month (S11), so 
the season-end offer on 23 Nov is a new, incremental 
entitlement, not a free benefit. A Monthly or Yearly holder who 
watches ATP is an engagement outcome, never a new ATP 
payer. Suppression: no bridge offer to anyone already holding 
a Monthly or Yearly pass.
Bridge 2: the rest of the portfolio to ATP (existing payers)
LaLiga late-night viewers get the Paris and Turin evening 
sessions (00:00-01:00 IST) with the morning replay; F1 holders 
get Tuesday-to-Friday night sessions, never race Sunday; 
MotoGP holders get Asian-swing morning sessions. Each is a 
first ATP purchase by an existing payer, reported apart from 
new payers, and each rung is credited toward the next. 
Entitlement is checked before every send: no offer to a user 
who already holds ATP access.
Controls, off-season and the three retention definitions
Caps: 3 pushes a week for new payers, 2 otherwise, 1 replay 
push a day; opt-out honoured platform-wide; a refund 
suppresses offers for 30 days. Off-season (23 Nov-4 Jan): 
Next Gen Finals (date TBD), "best of 2026" replays, no 
invented live tennis. Behavioural retention = repeat qualified 
viewing across distinct weeks; commercial retention = a paid 
ATP entitlement active in the window; renewal = repurchase 
after expiry, reported only for eligible matured cohorts.

The morning replay and the Race to Turin create the next occasion; the upgrade credit is the next paid decision. Outcomes: next-event return and the day-90 retained-payer 
rate, reported only for cohorts observed for 90 days. A still-valid pass proves nothing alone: entitlement and qualified viewing are reported together.

Definitions and method notes
Term
Definition used throughout the deck
New payer
First-ever FanCode payment is an ATP pass in the pilot window. A first ATP purchase by an existing F1, MotoGP, LaLiga or Monthly payer is an existing-payer ATP purchase, reported separately, never a new payer.
CAC, attributed vs incremental
Attributed CAC = row spend ÷ attributed new payers. Incremental CAC = spend ÷ incremental new payers (attributed × validated incremental share from the holdout or geo test). Blended CAC = spend ÷ deduplicated new 
payers; the harmonic form 1 ÷ Σ(wᵢ ÷ CACᵢ) is only a check across rows with the same denominator.
Contribution before marketing
Price ÷ 1.18 (18% GST assumed included) − 4% of gross (gateway 2%, support and refunds 2%) − ₹2 per assumed streaming hour. Excludes the fixed, confidential ATP rights fee, so it is not profit. Credits are price reductions, 
never revenue; a rupee of bundle revenue appears once.
