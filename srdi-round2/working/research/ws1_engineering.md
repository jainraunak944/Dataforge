### Q2. H2-ICE port-fuel-injection fundamentals (for an NA SI engine retrofit)

Important caveat: the two canonical reviews requested — Verhelst & Wallner, Prog. Energy Combust. Sci. 35 (2009) 490–527, and White, Steeper & Lutz, Int. J. Hydrogen Energy 31 (2006) 1292–1305 — could not be opened (all mirrors blocked: ScienceDirect, CORE, SciSpace, OSTI, Pacific Scholarly Commons, ResearchGate). Their specific numbers are therefore NOT quoted here. What follows is what accessible excerpts/summaries support.

(a) Air displacement and power loss
- Hydrogen (0.09 kg/m³ at 1 bar, 20 °C) occupies about 30 % of the mixture volume at stoichiometry in a PFI engine [S9a — IJHE 2024 DI-vs-PFI paper, excerpt]. Status: physical fact (modelled).
- "Even under stoichiometric conditions, hydrogen leads to an approximate 20 % loss in power output compared with a gasoline-fuelled SI engine" for PFI [S9a — search-summary of the same IJHE 2024 paper]. Status: reported.
- At λ = 2, torque/BMEP reductions of 34.9–40.7 % versus the optimal λ were reported, with DI 12.8–26.5 % better than PFI [S9b — search-summary; paper identity not verified]. Status: observed (single study, unverified).
- Power recovery routes: supercharging + EGR at stoichiometry (Verhelst et al., IJHE 2009, "Increasing the power output of hydrogen ICEs by means of supercharging and EGR" — title only, content not accessible) [S9c]; a 2023 review excerpt states a power output "higher than 30 % of gasoline" is achievable with a supercharged stoichiometric mixture with EGR (excerpt wording ambiguous; treat as ">gasoline by ~30 %" only after verification) [S9d].

(b) Brake thermal efficiency
- Reported in search-summaries (papers not opened): "maximum BTE 40.4 % at 2000 rpm with near-zero NOx" (a DI, e-turbocharged engine, ACS Omega 2024) and "BTE near 38 % with low NO" in lean PFI experiments [S9b]. Status: observed, single-engine studies, unverified. For a small NA PFI retrofit, expect the lower end; no accessible source gives part-load BTE for a ~1.2 L PFI H2 engine. NOT FOUND for part-load vs CNG comparison.

(c) Abnormal combustion
- Backfire (intake) is the dominant PFI-specific problem; a 2024 IJHE review is devoted to injection-control strategies to mitigate it (title confirmed, content not accessible) [S9e].
- IIT Delhi (L. M. Das): an appropriately designed timed manifold injection system can overcome backfire and rapid pressure rise; spark advance must be smaller than for gasoline; knock was observed when spark timing exceeded 26° CA BTDC in their test engine [S9f, S9g — search-summaries of IJHE 1991/2002 and OSTI ETDE record]. Status: observed (IIT Delhi test engines, 1990s–2000s).
- Hot-spot control (spark plug type, valve seats, oil-borne particulates), crankcase ventilation, lubricant selection: NOT FOUND in accessible text (this content is in Verhelst & Wallner §4–5 and White et al., which were blocked). Only accessible hint: Bosch's forthcoming H2 direct injector "requires no additional lubrication" [S10c].

(d) NOx versus lambda
- "Operating at λ = 1.3 produces maximum NOx, approximately 2.7 times higher than that produced at λ = 1" [S9h — Onorati et al. review PDF hosted by KAUST repository, excerpt]. Status: observed/reviewed.
- Search-summary of 2024–25 papers: NOx peaks at about λ ≈ 1.1 in one study and "decreases notably above λ 2.7, with levels below 10 ppm" [S9b]. Status: reported, unverified.
- The widely used design rule "λ ≥ ~2 keeps engine-out NOx very low" is consistent with the above but its canonical statement (Verhelst & Wallner Fig./§) could not be verified here.

(e) Materials, seals, lubrication, durability: NOT FOUND in accessible sources (see (c)). 

(f) EGR and aftertreatment
- Lean H2-ICE needs SCR (or LNT) for NOx: Toyota's Hydrogen HiAce prototype uses selective catalytic reduction to meet Euro VI [S12d]. Status: announced/observed (prototype).
- Stoichiometric operation with three-way catalyst + EGR is the alternative power-recovery route [S9c, S9d]. Quantitative conversion data: NOT FOUND.

Confidence: qualitative mechanisms — high; specific percentages — low-medium until the two reviews are re-verified from an accessible copy.

### Q4. Hydrogen storage facts

Density (compressed H2 at 15 °C): "The full-fill density of 35 MPa and 70 MPa tanks at 15 °C are respectively 24.0 g/L and 40.2 g/L" [S13a — Energies 2019, 12(4), 645, excerpt; matches the SAE J2601 100 % SOC definitions]. Cross-check: 39.75 kg/m³ at 700 barg, 20 °C [S13b]. NIST WebBook itself was blocked. Status: modelled (EOS).

Usable fraction / minimum pressure: quantitative rule NOT FOUND in an accessible standard. Search-summary of patents/industry text: on-board high-pressure regulators reduce 700 bar to "20 bar or less"; one patent describes stopping discharge at ~55 bar leaving ~5 % of the stored gas unusable; a 20 bar minimum allowable pressure is cited as a safety margin [S13c]. For a 350 bar system a 20 bar residual is ≈1.6 g/L ≈ 6.7 % of the 24.0 g/L full-fill mass (my computation, modelled). GTR 13 defines residual-pressure tests but the "usable" fraction is an OEM design choice.

Fast fill at 350 bar without pre-cooling: SAE J2601 (2020) applies to light-duty CHSS with total volume ≥ 49.7 L; tank material limit 85 °C; 3-min / 95–100 % SOC target is for T40 (−40 °C) dispensers; warmer delivery categories (T30, T20) and high ambient temperatures lengthen fills or reduce SOC; non-communication fills must stay within tight pre-cooling tolerances [S13d][S13e]. Achievable SOC for H35 with no pre-cooling: NOT FOUND (needs J2601 Table/ISO 19880-1 text). Note the ≥49.7 L scope threshold: a single-cylinder small-car retrofit could fall outside the LD protocol scope.

### Q6. Reuse of existing CNG components with pure hydrogen; HCNG

- ISO 11439 (CNG cylinders): hydrogen content in the gas is limited to 2 % by volume for cylinders made from steel with UTS > 950 MPa (0.1 % for wet gas) [S11a — search-summary of ISO 11439 FAQ text]. Indian Type 1 CNG cylinders (IS 15490 / ISO 11439, typically 34CrMo4) are therefore NOT qualified for pure hydrogen; hydrogen service cylinders are designed to ISO 19881 / UN R134 / GTR 13 with hydrogen-compatible steels, liners and elastomers. Specific UN R110 wording on hydrogen and ISO 19881 tensile-strength limits: NOT FOUND (unece.org, iso.org blocked).
- CNG regulators/injectors/hoses/valves: no accessible source evaluated their use with pure H2; embrittlement/permeation is the stated reason hydrogen components are separately certified (R134 vs R110). Treat as NOT reusable for H2 pending supplier confirmation. Status: inference from standards scope, not observed.
- HCNG (India): BIS specification IS 17314:2019 "Hydrogen-enriched CNG for automotive purposes" exists; MoRTH allowed H-CNG (18 % hydrogen mix) in CNG engines via a CMVR amendment notified in Sept 2020 (draft July 2020) [S11b][S11c]. Exact composition tolerances in IS 17314: NOT FOUND (BIS blocked).
- Trial results: IndianOil commissioned a compact-reformer HCNG plant at DTC Rajghat Depot-1 and began a 50-bus trial in Oct 2020 [S11d]. ARAI studies cited by the government: 18 % HCNG gives ~70 % lower CO and ~25 % lower HC in heavy-duty BS-IV engines vs CNG and 4–5 % better fuel economy [S11c]. The NPC/DOE HCNG topic paper reports an Indian (SIAM-led) trial selecting 18 % for the greatest NOx reduction with the lowest power loss: NMHC −58 %, THC −23 %, NOx −50 % [S11e — search-summary; document blocked]. Vehicle modification: sources state HCNG can be dispensed with minimal changes to CNG infrastructure; engine hardware changes are not described (calibration only implied) — explicit statement NOT FOUND.
- Energy share of H2 in an 18 vol % blend (my computation, modelled, standard property values: H2 LHV ≈ 10.8 MJ/Nm³, CH4 LHV ≈ 35.8 MJ/Nm³): 18×10.8 / (18×10.8 + 82×35.8) ≈ 6.2 % of energy. Hence HCNG-18 displaces only ~6 % of natural-gas energy — it is a NOx/CO measure, not a decarbonisation pathway.

Confidence: ISO 11439 2 % limit — medium (FAQ text, standard not opened); HCNG programme facts — medium; trial percentages — low-medium (press/summary).

- H2 Type III, 350 bar (Luxfer V060H35): OD 400 mm, 767 mm, 36 kg, 1.45 kg H2 [S2].
- H2 Type IV, 700 bar (Hexagon 53.4 L): OD 368 mm, 878 mm, 37 kg, 2.1 kg H2 [S1].
So the hydrogen cylinder is lighter but fatter (OD +40–80 mm) than the steel CNG cylinder it would replace, and holds 15–23 % of the fuel mass, i.e. ~35–55 % of the fuel energy (H2 120 MJ/kg vs CNG ~48–50 MJ/kg; modelled).

Mounting/crash requirements: UN R110 (CNG) — containers must withstand, when full, 20 g in the direction of travel and 8 g horizontally perpendicular for M1/N1 (10 g/5 g for M2/N2; 6.6 g/5 g for M3/N3), calculation permitted in lieu of test [S14d]. India: AIS-024 (components) and AIS-028 Ver. 4 (code of practice for CNG use; Ver. 5 adds Bio-CNG/LNG) [S14e]. Hydrogen: UN R134 / GTR 13 (container qualification, installation, TPRD venting, post-crash leak limits) and India AIS-195(A) which references GCR 2016, IS 16735, ISO 19881, R134, GTR 13 [S13n]. R134 acceleration figures and AIS-195 installation clauses: NOT FOUND (documents blocked).

Ground clearance: Tour S 163 mm [S3a/S6]. Underfloor mounting of a 368–440 mm OD cylinder is not credible on these platforms; boot mounting (as for CNG today) is the realistic option, at the cost of the boot (Tata's twin-cylinder underfloor layout for a 2 × 35 L CNG set frees ~200 L of boot [S5b] but relies on 2 × 35 L cylinders of small OD, which have no hydrogen equivalent found here).

Documented small-car hydrogen conversions with tank placement: NOT FOUND for India (only heavy trucks, 3-wheelers and a genset surfaced) [S12h–S12j]; internationally the bi-fuel demonstrators (Mazda RX-8: 110 L 350 bar tank in the boot; BMW Hydrogen 7: LH2 tank behind rear seats) show boot/rear placement [S12a][S12b].

Confidence: R110 g-loads — high (regulation text excerpt); cylinder dimensions — medium; Indian AIS/PESO detail — medium-low.

## 2. Contrary evidence / reasons an H2-ICE retrofit of a small Indian CNG car may not work

1. Power: PFI hydrogen displaces ~30 % of the intake air; stoichiometric PFI already loses ~20 % power versus gasoline [S9a], and lean operation at λ ≈ 2 (needed to keep NOx low without SCR) costs a further ~35–40 % [S9b]. For a 60 kW K12N this implies very roughly 30–35 kW at λ = 2 (my estimate, modelled) — below the CNG rating (~51–57 kW third-party) and marginal for a loaded Dzire/Ertiga taxi. Recovering power needs boost/EGR or stoichiometric TWC operation, which reintroduces backfire/pre-ignition risk and NOx.
2. Energy on board: the existing 55–60 L cylinder space holds ~1.3–1.45 kg H2 at 350 bar or ~2.1 kg at 700 bar [S1][S2][S13a] versus ~9–10 kg CNG, i.e. ~35 % (350 bar) to ~55 % (700 bar) of today's fuel energy. At the Tour S's declared 32 km/kg CNG (≈1.5 MJ/km) the equivalent hydrogen range is only ~100 km (350 bar) to ~170 km (700 bar) even before any H2-ICE efficiency penalty (modelled). The Toyota HiAce with 6.2 kg achieves "<200 km" [S12d].
3. Packaging: hydrogen cylinders are 40–80 mm larger in diameter than the 316–325 mm steel CNG cylinder [S1][S2][S14a][S14b]; no 350 bar Type IV cylinder in the 30–80 L class was found in accessible datasheets; no Indian PESO-approved car-size hydrogen cylinder was found [S13f]. Small tanks may also fall below SAE J2601's ≥49.7 L scope [S13d].
4. Nothing from the CNG kit carries over: ISO 11439/IS 15490 cylinders are limited to ≤2 % H2 [S11a]; regulators, valves, hoses and injectors need R134-type qualification; existing components are therefore sunk cost, not reused.
5. Abnormal combustion: backfire/pre-ignition in PFI H2 engines requires injection phasing after IVC, cold plugs, hot-spot control and crankcase management [S9e–S9g]; a bi-fuel fallback to CNG/petrol keeps the original hardware constraints, limiting how far the engine can be optimised.
6. Aftertreatment: lean H2 operation still needs SCR to meet Euro VI-class NOx (Toyota) [S12d]; adding urea SCR to a sub-₹8-lakh car is a cost and packaging burden.
7. Cost: DOE projections are $21/kWh at 10,000 units/yr (≈ $3,970 per 5.6 kg system, 2016$) [S13j]; at hundreds/yr the cost is undocumented but higher; plus injectors, regulator, OTV/TPRD, sensors, SCR and certification.
8. Refuelling: 350 bar fills without pre-cooling do not reach full SOC quickly (85 °C limit) [S13d][S13e]; India has no public hydrogen dispensing network for cars.
9. Evidence base: no passenger-car H2-ICE retrofit test data exist in India; international demonstrators (BMW, Mazda, Ford, Toyota HiAce) were all OEM-engineered, not aftermarket retrofits, and none reached series production.
