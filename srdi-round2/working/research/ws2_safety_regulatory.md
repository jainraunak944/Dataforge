## 1. Findings per question

### Q1. CNG/LPG retrofit precedent (CMVR Rule 115, AIS-024/025/026/028/137, approvals, H-CNG notification, IS 17314, vehicle age)

**Legal architecture of a retrofit in India (High confidence on structure; sub-rule numbering unverified).**
- Retrofit is an "alteration" under **Section 52 of the Motor Vehicles Act 1988**. CMVR **Rule 126** carries a proviso (text as reproduced in the commercial CMVR e-book, page 335, [S9]): *"PROVIDED ALSO that altered, retrofitted or adapted motor vehicles shall be tested and type approved by the testing agencies specified in this rule, or self-certified by original equipment manufacturers, or self-certified by the work-shops authorised by the State Government, in accordance with section 52 of the Act and rules made thereunder."* A 2026 amendment ("Central Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Rules, 2026") is reported to have **substituted several provisos to Rule 126 with immediate effect**, permitting self-certification for altered/retrofitted vehicles [S10]; the draft was circulated in 2025 (TeamLease article 52441). The exact 2026 GSR number and the final wording were **NOT FOUND**.
- **CMVR Rule 115** contains the CNG/LPG kit provisions (as summarised from indiankanoon Rule 115 / Rule 115B [S3], ARAI [S4], EVreporter/ARAI [S5]):
  - kits are "type approved for vehicles of specific make" and are fit for retrofitment within an engine-capacity band of **±7 % up to 1500 cc and ±5 % above 1500 cc**;
  - type approval of a CNG kit for retrofitment is **valid three years, renewable three years at a time**;
  - the test agency must complete the test and certify **within three months** of receiving the kit;
  - the kit manufacturer/supplier must submit a **layout plan for each model** to the test agency for vetting/approval;
  - retrofitment on in-use vehicles "shall be carried out by workshops authorised by the kit manufacturer/supplier or vehicle manufacturers";
  - the owner must apply to the registering authority **within 14 days** of the alteration (Section 52) for **endorsement in the RC** stating place/date of installation and installation-certificate number;
  - "Test procedure and safety guidelines for CNG vehicles, kit components including installation thereof, shall be as per AIS 024, as amended from time to time, till such time as corresponding BIS specifications are notified."
  - Test agencies: those notified under **Rule 126** (ARAI, ICAT, VRDE, CIRT, IIP, CFMTTI etc.) [S57].
  - The specific sub-rule numbers (e.g. 115(11)–(18)) could not be confirmed. **UNRESOLVED.**
- **BS-VI retrofit**: MoRTH **G.S.R. 625(E) dated 11 August 2022** allows retrofitment of CNG and LPG kits on BS-VI petrol vehicles and replacement of diesel engines with CNG/LPG engines for BS-VI vehicles **< 3.5 t GVW** (draft 27 Jan 2022) [S6]. Confidence: High.
- **AIS standards** (titles only; hmr.araiindia.com blocked): AIS-024 and AIS-028 are published together as "AIS-024 (Version 4) / AIS-028 (Version 4)" [S7]; GSR 585(E) refers to "AIS 024, AIS-028 Revision 1". AIS-025/026 (LPG) exist by title only in this session — **content NOT FOUND**. Emission procedure for retrofits: a regulatory update for Nov–Dec 2022 lists "Retrofitment of CNG/LPG Kit – Draft Amd 1 to AIS-137 (Part 9)" [S58], indicating AIS-137 Part 9 is the emission-test procedure used for retrofit kits (Low confidence; not verified).
- **Approval cost**: an industry statement quoted by Autocar Professional puts CNG-kit type approval at **Rs 40–50 lakh, valid three years** [S8]. This is an industry claim, not an ARAI/ICAT fee schedule. **Official fee schedule NOT FOUND.**
- **H-CNG notification**: MoRTH **G.S.R. 585(E) dated 25 September 2020** amended CMVR Rule 115 to include H-CNG as an automotive fuel [S1]. Reported inserted wording: *"Specifications for commercial Hydrogen Enriched Compressed Natural Gas (HCNG) shall be as per IS 17314:2019 and as amended from time to time"* and *"The CNG/Bio-CNG/HCNG Vehicles shall meet all the safety requirements as per AIS 024, AIS-028 Revision 1 as applicable."* Confidence: High that GSR 585(E)/25-09-2020 exists and references IS 17314:2019; Medium on exact wording.
- **IS 17314:2019**: BIS specification of H-CNG for automotive purposes [S2]. The "18 % hydrogen" figure appears in PIB/press summaries ("H-CNG (18 % mix of hydrogen)"); **the standard's own composition clause was not seen** — confirm the 18 % (by volume) limit from the BIS text before citing. Confidence: Medium.
- **PESO/cylinders for CNG**: RC-endorsement checklists require a "CNG cylinder certificate issued by Department of Explosives (PESO), Nagpur" and periodic hydro-test certificates [S54]; GCR 2016 (2025 amendment) sets **3-yearly** hydrostatic/hydrostatic-stretch testing for CNG, CBG and on-board hydrogen cylinders [S24].
- **Maximum vehicle age for retrofit**: **no central CMVR age cap for CNG retrofit was found.** Practical caps come from (a) NCR restrictions on petrol vehicles ≥ 15 years and diesel ≥ 10 years (which also apply after CNG conversion), and (b) Rule 88 age limits for commercial vehicles under national permits (12/15 years; MoRTH draft of 18 Aug 2026 proposes +5 years for EV/hydrogen/CNG CVs) [S22]. Confidence: Medium (state-level practice), NOT FOUND for a central retrofit-specific age rule.

### Q7. Hazard data

- **Properties (H2Tools basic properties chart [S43])**: LFL **4.0 vol %**, UFL **75.0 vol %**; lower detonation limit **18.3 %**, upper **59.0 %**; minimum ignition energy **0.017 mJ**; autoignition temperature **585 °C** (atmospheric pressure). Hydrogen is colourless/odourless and its flame is nearly invisible in daylight; buoyancy, diffusivity and small molecular size make it hard to contain but also make it dilute quickly outdoors. **DOE fact sheet "Hydrogen: Similar but Different" [S44]**: diffusivity 3.8× natural gas; MIE about 5 % of that of natural gas; flammability range about five times wider than methane's.
  - *Comparison values for CNG/petrol/LPG were not captured from the indexed sources in this session. General reference values (UNSOURCED here — verify against the H2Tools "Hydrogen compared to other fuels" table before use): methane LFL–UFL ≈ 5–15 %, MIE ≈ 0.28 mJ, autoignition ≈ 540 °C; propane 2.1–9.5 %, MIE ≈ 0.25 mJ, autoignition ≈ 470 °C; gasoline vapour 1.4–7.6 %, MIE ≈ 0.24 mJ, autoignition ≈ 230–280 °C.*
- **Enclosed spaces / workshops**: **NFPA 2 (2023)** requires repair garages for hydrogen-fuelled vehicles to have approved exhaust ventilation per its Chapter 6, with mechanical or fixed natural ventilation of **≥ 1 scf/min/ft² (0.0051 m³/s/m²)** of floor area over the area of storage/use, inlets low on walls and outlets at the high point of the room, and fuelling-system shutdown on ventilation failure [S45]. **ISO/TR 15916:2015** (basic considerations for the safety of hydrogen systems) has been superseded by **ISO/TS 15916:2026** [S46]. UK HSE: motor-vehicle-repair guidance HSG261 and an HSE "Hydrogen safety" hub exist; the GOV.UK note "Construction and use of hydrogen-powered vehicles" is marked **withdrawn** [S47]. An HSE case study addresses hydrogen vehicles in tunnels/confined spaces (risk must be "the same or lower than for fossil-fuel transport").
- **TPRD/venting**: GTR 13 Phase 2 clarified TPRD discharge-direction rules (not towards exposed electrical terminals, passenger compartment, wheel housings, etc.) [S41]; GCR 2025 requires nitrogen purging of vent stacks when venting hydrogen [S24]. Detailed AIS-195 venting clauses NOT captured.
- **Incidents**:
  - **Kjørbo (Sandvika), Norway, 10 June 2019** — Uno-X/Nel station: root cause identified by Nel with Gexcon as an **assembly error of a specific plug in a high-pressure storage tank — inner bolts not adequately torqued (human error) → leak → ignition**; Nel launched inspection/integrity verification of similar plugs and new assembly/verification/documentation procedures [S48].
  - **Santa Clara, California, 1 June 2019** — Air Products trailer transfill facility: tube-trailer at ~95 % full; leak near fill-line isolation valve; **unauthorised repair by a driver and miscommunication between two drivers re-opened a pneumatic valve into a disconnected manifold**; no injuries; blast felt several km away [S49].
  - **Gangneung, South Korea, 23 May 2019** — 40 m³, 10 bar buffer tank at a test facility exploded (2 dead, 6 injured) after **oxygen ingress into the hydrogen tank** (electrolyser project, not a vehicle) [S50]. **Busan, 27 Dec 2024** — HRS fire from a leak after safety-valve activation [S50].
  - **On-road hydrogen passenger-vehicle fires/tank ruptures**: none identified in this session; the HIAD database exists for systematic search [S50].
- **Crash tests**: **Toyota Mirai (2021) Euro NCAP 5 stars** — adult occupant 88 %, child 85 %, vulnerable road users 80 %, safety assist 82 %; Euro NCAP stated the hydrogen powertrain "had no effect on its inherent safety". **Hyundai Nexo (2018) Euro NCAP 5 stars**, first FCEV tested [S51].
- **DOE NOx page**: the requested URL (energy.gov/cmei/fuels/...) was not found; the DOE page "Does the use of hydrogen produce air pollutants such as nitrogen oxides?" is indexed at energy.gov/eere/fuelcells/… [S52]. Reported content: fuel cells produce no NOx; **any high-temperature combustion (diesel, gasoline, natural gas, hydrogen) can produce NOx**; pure-hydrogen combustion burns hotter than natural gas and "may result in comparably higher NOx", which "may be offset" by hydrogen's wider stable combustion range allowing leaner air/fuel ratios that cool the flame. (Directly relevant: an H2-ICE conversion must demonstrate NOx control — lean-burn/EGR/SCR — because the H2 fuel does not remove the NOx pathway.)

### Q8. Registration, insurance, warranty

- **RC endorsement**: **Form 22D "[Refer rule 47B(1)] Endorsement of alteration/retrofitment"** (parivahan.gov.in) is the application for recording an alteration/retrofit in the RC [S54]. Documents typically demanded for CNG: RC, insurance, post-installation PUC, installer invoice, kit type-approval certificate, retrofitter authorisation letter, AIS-024/028 checklist, PESO cylinder certificate, approved layout drawings; processing 10–30 days [S54]. Section 52 requires the application within 14 days [S3].
- **Hydrogen registration mark**: G.S.R. 819(E), 3 Nov 2025 — display of registration mark for hydrogen-fuel vehicles "for easy identification" (colour scheme details not captured) [S20].
- **Insurance**: India Motor Tariff endorsement **IMT-25** covers "loss and/or damage to the CNG/LPG kit fitted in the vehicle insured arising from an accidental loss or damage to the vehicle insured, subject to the limit of the Insured's Declared Value of the CNG/LPG kit specified in the Schedule"; insurers report a **Rs 60 additional third-party premium** for bi-fuel kits and a proportional own-damage loading; an undeclared/un-endorsed kit can invalidate claims [S53]. **No IMT endorsement for hydrogen kits exists** (inference from the IMT wording being CNG/LPG-specific).
- **OEM warranty**: secondary sources citing Maruti Suzuki's owner's manual state that vehicles retrofitted with (aftermarket) CNG kits are excluded from warranty coverage; factory-fitted CNG retains warranty [S55]. Not verified against the manual text.
- **Inference for H2 conversions** (labelled inference): (a) an RC endorsement under Rule 47B/Form 22D would require a type-approved kit and an authorised installer — neither category exists for hydrogen today; (b) a new IMT endorsement or insurer-specific add-on would be needed; (c) OEM powertrain warranty would be void; (d) the hydrogen registration-mark scheme of GSR 819(E) would presumably apply after conversion.

### Q9. Route for prototype/trial vehicles on public roads

- **Trade certificate (CMVR Rules 33–42)**: Rule 41 limits use of a vehicle under a trade certificate to: "(a) for test, by or on behalf of the holder of a trade certificate during the course of, or after completion of, construction or repair; (b) for proceeding to or returning from a weigh bridge or for registration; (c) for a reasonable trial or demonstration by or for the benefit of a prospective purchaser; (d) ... delivery; (e) ... workshop for fitting a body, painting or repairs" [S56]. This is the standard mechanism for OEM/test-agency road testing of prototypes (Medium confidence; verbatim as indexed).
- **Rule 126**: manufacturers/importers submit prototypes to notified test agencies (ARAI, VRDE, CIRT, ICAT, IIP, CFMTTI etc.) [S57]; Rule 126 provisos also deem compliance for imported CBUs "for the purpose of personal use, demonstration, **testing, research or scientific work**" via a certificate of compliance to EEC/ECE/Japanese standards [S9] (this is how imported hydrogen demonstrators such as the Mirai can be registered).
- **Precedents**: **Toyota Mirai pilot, launched 16 March 2022** by the MoRTH minister — TKM with **ICAT** "to study and evaluate" the FCEV on Indian roads and climate [S35]. **Tata Motors hydrogen heavy-truck trials flagged off 4 March 2025** by the MoRTH and MNRE ministers — 16 vehicles (H2-ICE and FCEV), up to **24 months**, on freight routes around Mumbai, Pune, Delhi-NCR, Surat, Vadodara, Jamshedpur, Kalinganagar [S36]. No MoRTH "permission letter" text was found; the enabling instruments are the trade-certificate/test-agency route plus MNRE pilot sanction.
- **MNRE pilot scheme (transport)**: Scheme Guidelines notified **14 February 2024**, outlay **Rs 496 crore to FY 2025-26**; objective "to support the deployment of green hydrogen as fuel in **buses and trucks**, evaluate their economic viability and performance as well as demonstrate safe operations of hydrogen fuelling stations"; steering committee co-chaired by Secretary MoRTH and Secretary MNRE [S33]. As told to Parliament (25 March 2025): **5 pilot projects, 37 vehicles (15 fuel-cell, 22 H2-ICE), 9 HRS, 10 routes, Rs 208 crore support**, awarded to Tata Motors, Reliance, NTPC, ANERT, Ashok Leyland, HPCL, BPCL, IOCL, following recommendations of ARAI and MoRTH [S34]. **Passenger cars are not within the stated objective** — a car-conversion pilot would need a separate sanction or a state/industry-funded route.
- Practical "controlled trial before public deployment" description supported by the evidence: (1) build converted prototypes under an OEM/kit-maker trade certificate; (2) engage a Rule 126 test agency (ARAI/ICAT) for an evaluation programme against AIS-195 (by analogy) and AIS-024/028 installation practice; (3) obtain PESO approval for the on-board cylinder design and a Form-H licence for the dispensing point; (4) seek MoRTH/MNRE steering-committee acknowledgement (as done for the Tata trials); (5) only then apply for a retrofit-kit type approval and RC endorsement under Section 52/Rule 126/Rule 47B once a hydrogen retrofit standard exists.

## 3. Contrary evidence / regulatory blockers

1. **No approval route exists today for an aftermarket hydrogen conversion of an M1 car.** AIS-195/Rule 125M is a new-vehicle type-approval instrument; Rule 115's retrofit-kit machinery is written for CNG/LPG (and HCNG only as a fuel on CNG vehicles). Nothing found in 2023–2026 MoRTH/MNRE/PESO output addresses hydrogen retrofit. Any claim of "approvable" must be phrased as "would require a new AIS/Rule 115 amendment or a test-agency-led pilot approval".
2. **AIS-195 retrofit exclusion/inclusion is unknown** because the document could not be read; the submission must not assert either way.
3. **Cylinder supply**: PESO Type IV approvals exist only for large (150–250 L) CV cylinders; a car-package cylinder (≈ 30–60 L water volume, 350 bar) with PESO approval was not found. GCR 3-year hydrotest and 15-year life apply.
4. **SAE J2601 tables start at 49.7 L CHSS** — small car conversions may fall outside standard light-duty fuelling tables (needs a station-side protocol decision).
5. **Refuelling infrastructure**: PESO Form-H licensing only since May 2025; no public retail HRS; the MNRE pilot stations serve buses/trucks on 10 routes.
6. **Safety physics**: MIE 0.017 mJ, 4–75 % flammability, 18–59 % detonation range, invisible flame, and buoyant accumulation in enclosed spaces (garages, basements, home parking) — the CNG-workshop model (open sheds, no H2 detection) does not transfer without NFPA 2-type ventilation/detection and PESO-trained staff.
7. **NOx**: hydrogen combustion still forms thermal NOx (DOE); a conversion must carry a NOx-control strategy and an emission-test procedure that does not yet exist for H2-ICE retrofits in AIS-137.
8. **Commercial**: OEM warranty void; no IMT insurance endorsement; RC endorsement impossible without a type-approved kit; state age caps (NCR 15-year petrol) limit the addressable fleet.
9. **Policy tailwinds are CV-focused** (permit exemption July 2026; age-limit extension draft Aug 2026; MNRE pilots for buses/trucks) — none targets passenger-car conversions.
