Small-car glider manufacturing emissions in t CO2e: NOT FOUND numerically; proxy 4–6 t CO2e for a ~900 kg ICE car and +2–3 t for a 25–30 kWh LFP pack (B: 27 kWh × 60–75 kg/kWh = 1.6–2.0 t).

**(e) H2-ICE NOx and limits** — BS-VI M1 (positive-ignition incl. petrol; understood to apply equally to CNG): NOx 60 mg/km; diesel 80 mg/km [S71]; Euro 7 sets a fuel-neutral 60 mg/km [S70]. Measured/modelled H2-ICE: a 4-cylinder DI hydrogen engine at λ = 2.4–3.0 (BTE up to 43%) achieved WLTC tailpipe NOx < 35 mg/km with urea-SCR (≈100% conversion at suitable temperature, NH3/NOx 1.1–1.3) [S68]; ETH/Bosch simulation of a PFI-type H2 passenger car shows engine-out NOx spikes above the ultra-lean regime at >1 m/s² accelerations and highway speeds, so a non-hybrid H2 vehicle cannot hold "extremely low" engine-out NOx in normal driving — hybridisation or aftertreatment is needed; a lean-NOx catalyst/SCR is the conventional answer [S70]. A 55 kW 4-cylinder PFI H2 engine study (NRMM) reports tailpipe NOx variants with/without aftertreatment but values were not accessible [S69]. Model assumption (C): engine-out 50–200 mg/km lean PFI without aftertreatment; <35–60 mg/km with SCR/lean-NOx trap; budget an aftertreatment line item.

Hydrogen's indirect GHG effect: GWP100 ≈ 11 ± 4, GWP20 ≈ 37 ± 18 (multi-model; Ocko & Hamburg 2022 is the seminal paper — its exact values could not be verified in this session and one search summary garbled them) [S72]; a 2024 study argues OH-chemistry biases may overstate the GWP by ~20% [S72]. Supply-chain leakage assumptions in literature 1–10% [S72]. For completeness: at 3% leakage, 11 kg CO2e/kg-H2 GWP100 → +0.33 kg CO2e per kg used (+6 g/km at 57 km/kg) (B).

**(f) Carbon-credit price evidence** — Indian voluntary-market averages quoted at ₹1,350/t (Jan 2026), ₹1,650 (Mar) and ₹2,800 (Jul 2026) by a project developer's price tracker [S73, low grade]; MSCI global carbon-credit price index averaged $3.50/t in 2025 ($4.30 in 2024) [S73]; India's CCTS offset mechanism (launched Dec 2023) runs alongside private standards [S73, CEEW]. Round-1's $10–15/t is above the observed global average; use $3.5/t low, ₹1,500/t (~$17) base-case-high, and treat credits as upside only.

## 3. Contrary evidence / reasons the economics may not work

1. **Delivered hydrogen price gap.** CEEW's India model gives delivered green hydrogen ≈ USD 4.2/kg (~₹360/kg) in 2030 at a 1,000 kg/day station with 30% utilisation, and states today's small-network refuelling costs exceed USD 10/kg [S22]. Round 1 assumed ₹160–200/kg at the pump by 2030 — that is roughly the *production-only* cost projected by IEA/CEEW for 2030 ($2–3/kg) [S22][S57] with little or no dispensing adder. At ₹360/kg and 57.5 km/kg, hydrogen costs ₹6.3/km vs CNG ₹3.3/km (₹87/kg, 26 km/kg) and BEV ₹1–2.5/km. Even the ammonia-implied ₹302/kg for the hydrogen molecule (before compression/dispensing) is ~₹5.2/km [S38, B].
2. **Utilisation trap.** Small stations at 20–30% utilisation carry $6–9/kg of station cost alone [S46][S48]; the capital-recovery share falls only with throughput. A 20-car pilot at 2 kg/car/day (≈40 kg/day) cannot reach the 200–500 kg/day break-even quoted for Indian stations [S56].
3. **Foreign pump prices are a warning, not a bound to beat:** California $32–36/kg [S50], Germany €10–15/kg [S51], Japan ¥1,650/kg [S52] after a decade of subsidy.
4. **Tank cost floor.** Even at 10,000 systems/yr the Type IV system is ≈ $710/kg H2 (2016$) [S2]; a 1.5-kg tank cannot reach the DOE $300–333/kg targets [S4]; carbon-fibre is the dominant and slowly falling cost [S1]. The kit price is therefore unlikely to fall below ₹1.5 lakh without volumes in the tens of thousands.
5. **CNG's own economics are strong and improving in regulation:** CNG small cars moved to 18% GST (Sep 2025) [S24]; CEEW finds CNG the lowest-TCO option for lower-mileage 4W and second only to EV for taxis [S22]. Rising CNG prices (spot-RLNG driven, +₹2/kg in Mumbai Sep 2026 [S17]) help hydrogen only if hydrogen is not itself gas-derived.
6. **BEV headwinds for hydrogen:** EV taxis are "substantially cheaper" on TCO [S22]; 8-yr/160,000-km battery warranties and ~2%/yr degradation remove the early-replacement argument [S26][S27]; Delhi waives road tax/registration for BEVs to 2030 [S25]; DC charging at ₹16–24/kWh gives ₹2.4–3.6/km even on public fast chargers [S21] — still below hydrogen at any 2030 delivered price found.
7. **Emissions can be worse than CNG if hydrogen is not truly green:** grid-electrolysis hydrogen on the 2025 Indian grid ≈ 35–43 kg CO2/kg → ~0.6–0.75 kg CO2/km (B), 6× a CNG car; grey SMR hydrogen 10–14 kg/kg → 175–245 g/km, above CNG's tailpipe ~104 g/km (B). The claim only works with certified ≤2 kg CO2e/kg hydrogen [S65]. ICCT finds FCEVs on grey hydrogen only 26% better than gasoline, while CNG is 13% better [S63].
8. **NOx compliance is not free:** a non-hybrid H2 vehicle leaves the ultra-lean regime under normal accelerations, so lean-NOx aftertreatment (SCR/LNT) or load limiting is needed to hold 60 mg/km [S70][S68]; this adds cost and needs durability proof under AIS-137 [S80].
9. **Hydrogen leakage** (GWP100 ≈ 11) erodes but does not eliminate the benefit at realistic 1–3% leakage [S72]; at 10% leakage it becomes material.
10. **Policy fit:** MNRE transport pilots so far fund only buses/trucks [S54][S55]; state incentives are written for fuel-cell vehicles (Maharashtra) or buses/stations (Gujarat) [S76][S78]; Delhi's 2026 policy excludes even hybrids [S25]. Carbon-credit revenue at ₹1,500/t (~$17) or the $3.5/t global average is ≤ ₹0.2/km — immaterial [S73].
11. **Fuel price volatility cuts both ways:** the 2026 crude/LNG shock lifted petrol to ₹102–111/L and CNG to ₹87–94/kg [S19][S17], improving hydrogen's relative position, but also raised grey hydrogen (RMI: $3–7.5/kg under LNG disruption) [S42] and the gas-linked power inputs.
