### 2.2 Delhi Motor Vehicle Aggregator and Delivery Service Provider Scheme, 2023 (EV transition)
Notified by GNCTD on **21 Nov 2023** [S17]. Text visible via Legitquest reproduction, Clause 6 (aggregators) and Clause 8 (delivery service providers) — cumulative share of **newly onboarded** fleet that must be electric:

| Milestone from notification | Three-wheeler (passenger) | Four-wheeler (passenger) |
|---|---|---|
| Within two years | 50% | 25% |
| Within three years | 75% | 50% |
| Within four years | 100% | 75% |
| Within five years | — | 100% |

Additional provisions reported by Hindustan Times at notification (30 Nov 2023): early milestones of "5% within six months, 25% within a year" for four-wheelers and "only EVs" for newly onboarded two-wheelers [S17]. The 6-month/1-year milestones were **not visible in the reproduced text** and should be checked against the Gazette notification before quoting. The scheme also states the objective that all aggregator fleets be fully electric by 2030 (Transport Dept announcement) [S17]. Implication for H2-SHIFT: in Delhi, a **CNG car already in an aggregator fleet is not displaced**, but the compliance ratio applies to new onboarding; an H2-converted CNG car is not an "electric vehicle" under the scheme and would not count toward the EV ratio. This is a hard constraint for the Delhi aggregator segment (but not for institutional/contract fleets, tourist permits, or NCR-Haryana/UP fleets where CNG remains permitted for new onboarding [S19]).

### 2.5 EV fleet operators: BluSmart collapse and successors
- **BluSmart**: suspended services in Delhi-NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru in April 2025 after SEBI's **15 Apr 2025 interim order** against Gensol Engineering (which owned/leased most of BluSmart's EVs). SEBI found promoter fund diversion (≈₹978 crore of EV-purchase loans diverted; "complete breakdown of internal controls"), barred the Jaggi brothers from board positions and ordered a forensic audit. Root cause per Down To Earth analysis: the **asset-heavy leasing model** (fleet owned by a related-party lessor) plus promoter governance failure — not EV technology per se [S23].
- **Everest Fleet** (Uber-backed): ~20,500 cars, ~2,000 EVs (May 2025); target 10,000 EVs by 2026; in talks to absorb BluSmart EVs for Uber Green [S24].
- **Refex eVeelz**: 2,000+ EV cabs across 7 cities; Uber partnership to deploy 1,000 EVs by 2026 [S24].
- **Evera Cabs**: onboarding 500 ex-BluSmart EVs; plan 2,500 EVs by Mar 2026 and 4,500 by Jun 2026 [S24].
- **Uber–Tata Motors**: Feb 2023 agreement for 25,000 Xpres-T EVs across seven cities (largest fleet EV commitment; delivery status 2026 NOT FOUND) [S25].
- Reported structural issues for EV cabs: financing, depot charging at scale, and EVs "yet to be affordable for Indian drivers" (driver-owner model) [S24].

### 4.6 Standards and permits (station and vehicle)
- PIB backgrounder (May 2024): 34 new hydrogen-related standards issued in 14 months after NGHM launch; agencies: OISD, PESO, PNGRB, MoRTH, BIS. PESO handles storage/handling under the Gas Cylinder Rules 2016 and SMPV(U) Rules 2016 and is harmonising ISO 19880 (HRS) with BIS; PNGRB/PESO stakeholder session on hydrogen transmission held 15 Mar 2024 [S43].
- Vehicle side: Central Motor Vehicles (Fourth Amendment) Rules 2025 add a registration-mark colour scheme for hydrogen-fuelled vehicles [S43]. MoRTH draft (18 Aug 2026): +5-year operational age for EV, hydrogen and CNG commercial vehicles and eased national-permit rules [S43].
- **Retrofit approval pathway for CNG→H2 conversion of in-use cars: NOT FOUND.** CNG retrofits rely on AIS-024 and RFC approval [S16]; no equivalent AIS/CMVR route for aftermarket hydrogen conversion kits was identified. This is a licensing gap that H2-SHIFT must address explicitly (type approval of kit + PESO cylinder approval + RTO endorsement).

## 8. Contrary evidence / reasons H2-SHIFT may not work (as evidenced in this workstream)

1. **No hydrogen retail network and no cars in the national pilots.** India's verified dispensing points are three captive sites (Faridabad, Vadodara, Leh); the MNRE pilot portfolio is 27 buses + 43 trucks on 21 corridors, with ~10% of funds released. Nothing in the 2024–26 pipeline creates public 350/700-bar dispensing for light vehicles in Delhi-NCR, Gujarat or Pune before 2028 [S36][S37][S46].
2. **Fuel price gap.** Observed delivered hydrogen prices are €8–13/kg in Germany and $32–36/kg in California; India's best refinery-gate tender is ₹279/kg untaxed, before compression and dispensing. Delhi–Pune CNG is ₹87–98/kg and 1 kg H2 displaces only ≈2.4–2.5 kg CNG in energy terms, so per-km fuel parity needs ≈₹220–240/kg H2 at the nozzle — not reachable from any observed price [S7][S8][S39][S40][S41].
3. **Policy in the target cities points the other way.** Delhi's aggregator scheme (Nov 2023) forces new-onboarding EV ratios up to 100% in five years and Delhi EV Policy 2026 shifts autos (2027) and two-wheelers (2028) to EV-only registration; an H2-converted CNG car earns no EV credit or incentive [S17][S35]. CAQM allows only CNG/EV for new NCR aggregator vehicles from Jan 2026 — this preserves CNG but not hydrogen [S19].
4. **Global light-duty hydrogen is contracting**: Stellantis cancelled its H2 vans (Jul 2025), Shell exited California LD stations (Feb 2024), H2 Mobility closed 36 German car stations in 2025, OMV closed all Austrian stations, GM–Honda's FC plant winds down in 2026, Mirai US sales fell to 210 in 2025, Europe sold 14 FCEVs in H1 2026 [S53][S64][S65][S66][S67][S51].
5. **The precedent most like H2-SHIFT (HCNG in Delhi) did not scale**: the 50-bus, 18% HCNG trial (2020) reported CO/HC cuts but no NOx benefit and no published follow-on deployment [S47].
6. **BEV taxi comparators are cheap and improving**: Tiago EV from ₹6.99 lakh, Punch EV from ₹9.69 lakh, Xpres-T fleet EV ₹13.04 lakh, Delhi depot power ₹4.50/kWh, public DC ₹18–25/kWh, 5% GST and full road-tax waiver to 2030; real-world 12.8–15.3 kWh/100 km gives a fuel cost of roughly ₹0.6–0.7/km at depot tariff versus ≈₹2.5–3/km for CNG at 30–35 km/kg (indicative arithmetic) [S27][S30][S32][S33][S35].
7. **The fleet-EV failure (BluSmart) was governance, not technology** — so it does not support a "BEVs failed, try hydrogen" narrative; the same lessors/operators are re-deploying the EVs [S23][S24].
8. **Regulatory pathway for aftermarket hydrogen conversion of cars does not exist** (no AIS-024 equivalent); PESO cylinder/station approvals and CMVR rules are oriented to OEM hydrogen vehicles and heavy-duty pilots [S16][S43].
9. **Suzuki's own India roadmap is CNG + biogas/CBG**, with hydrogen limited to small-engine research and concepts — H2-SHIFT is not aligned with a stated product plan of the sponsor [S68][S58].
10. **Uncertainties on the CNG side too**: CNG prices rose sharply in 2026 on imported spot LNG costs (Middle East crisis) and PNGRB itself faces expert scepticism on 2030 gas targets [S7][S77][S76]; growth of the factory-CNG parc is nevertheless strong (Maruti >7 lakh/yr), which supports the *CNG-retention* leg (HCNG blend, fallback) more than the hydrogen leg.
